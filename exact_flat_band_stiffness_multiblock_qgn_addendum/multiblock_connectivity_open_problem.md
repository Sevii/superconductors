# The missing multi-block connectivity criterion: a precise statement of the open problem

*Expansion of a point raised in the 2026-07-24 review of `multiblock_qgn_addendum.md`.*

## 1. Why MB3 is the H4 of Paper 2

The parent paper's architecture had a deliberate two-step structure. Theorem 1 was proved under H4 (a simple analytic ground branch), and then Proposition 1 *discharged* H4 for the projected PSD-Hubbard subclass by converting it into checkable microscopic structure: the AGP is the unique zero mode in every nonsingular filling sector **iff** the projected-frame overlap graph is connected. Everything unconditional in the paper — and everything in the two new connectivity documents — flows through Proposition 1. It is the hinge that turns "a theorem under hypotheses" into "a theorem about Model I and Model II."

The multiblock addendum currently has the analogue of Theorem 1 but not the analogue of Proposition 1. Two hypotheses are assumed rather than derived:

- **MB3** (for Theorem 5.1): in each fixed composition sector \(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_q)\), the product AGP \(|\mathbf n\rangle\) is the unique zero mode.
- **The zero-manifold hypothesis** (for Theorem 8.1): at fixed total pair number, the kernel of \(H(0)\) is *exactly* \(\operatorname{span}\{|\mathbf n\rangle\}\) — no zero modes outside the product-AGP span.

Section 10 of the addendum lists "whether block-resolved irreducibility holds" among the remaining model-dependent questions, but does not state what the criterion should look like. It has a clean conjectural form, and both failure modes are identifiable in advance. That is what makes it a well-posed open problem rather than a vague desideratum.

## 2. The class, and what Proposition 3.1 already forces

Proposition 3.1 forces every unperturbed null matrix \(s_\lambda\) to be block diagonal:

\[
s_\lambda=\bigoplus_{a=1}^q p_\lambda^a,
\qquad p_\lambda^a=(p_\lambda^a)^\dagger \ \text{acting on } \mathcal V_a .
\]

This has an immediate structural consequence for what a "multi-block projected Hubbard model" can even be: a rank-one Hubbard factor \(|v_x\rangle\langle v_x|\) whose frame vector straddles two singular blocks is *not* block diagonal, so it cannot commute with the weighted pairing operator. The admissible factors are direct sums of per-block pieces — in the natural rank-one case, a *family of blockwise frames* \(\{v_\lambda^a\}\), one per block, sharing the label \(\lambda\). The natural normalization is the blockwise tight-frame (Parseval) condition \(\sum_\lambda p_\lambda^a = c_a I_{\mathcal V_a}\), the analogue of \(\sum_x p_x=I_{\mathcal V}\) in (10a) of the parent.

The key point: the blocks are *not* independent, because the same factor index \(\lambda\) couples them. The zero-mode problem in a composition sector is the joint commutant of the operators

\[
A_\lambda=\bigoplus_a d\Gamma_{n_a}(p_\lambda^a)
\]

acting on \(\bigotimes_a \operatorname{End}(\Lambda^{n_a}\mathcal V_a)\) after blockwise vectorization. Whether the product AGP is unique is a question about the \(*\)-algebra \(\mathcal A\) generated by \(\{s_\lambda\}\) inside the block-diagonal algebra \(\bigoplus_a\operatorname{End}(\mathcal V_a)\).

## 3. The conjectured criterion

**Conjecture (multi-block Proposition 1).** For blockwise-tight rank-one frames as above, the following are equivalent:

1. The product AGP is the unique zero mode in every composition sector \(0\le n_a\le L_a\) (i.e., MB3 holds everywhere), and the fixed-total-number kernel is exactly the span of the product AGPs.
2. \(\mathcal A=\bigoplus_a\operatorname{End}(\mathcal V_a)\).
3. Two conditions hold jointly:
   - **(intra-block connectivity)** for each \(a\), the block-\(a\) frame graph \(\lambda\sim\mu \iff \langle v_\lambda^a,v_\mu^a\rangle\ne0\) is connected on its active labels and the frame spans \(\mathcal V_a\);
   - **(inter-block inequivalence)** for no pair \(a\ne b\) does there exist a unitary \(W:\mathcal V_a\to\mathcal V_b\) with \(Wp_\lambda^aW^\dagger=p_\lambda^b\) for every \(\lambda\).

The equivalence of 2 and 3 is Wedderburn/Jacobson-density bookkeeping: a proper \(*\)-subalgebra of a direct sum of matrix algebras fails in exactly one of two ways — some block acts reducibly (which, for rank-one frames, is precisely frame-graph disconnection, by the parent's spanning-tree Lie induction), or two blocks carry unitarily equivalent representations. So the conjecture says the *only* new failure mode beyond Paper 1's disconnection is **synchronization** of two blocks' frames.

Two remarks that make the conjecture do real work:

**(a) It upgrades Theorem 8.1 for free.** Because each \(s_\lambda\) is block diagonal, \(H(0)\) commutes with every block charge \(N_a\), and a zero mode is annihilated by the whole Lie algebra generated by the factors \(d\Gamma_\uparrow(p_\lambda^a)-d\Gamma_\downarrow(\bar p_\lambda^a)\). Per-block connectivity puts \(iI_{\mathcal V_a}\) in that Lie algebra (parent's induction), so every zero mode satisfies \(N_{a\uparrow}=N_{a\downarrow}\) *blockwise* — imbalanced and odd-charge sectors are excluded, and the fixed-total-number kernel decomposes into composition sectors, where MB3 finishes the job. So proving the sector statement automatically delivers the stronger zero-manifold hypothesis that Theorem 8.1 needs. One criterion closes both assumptions.

**(b) Synchronization is not excluded by Remark 3.3.** The merge rule combines subspaces with *equal singular values* into one block. Two blocks with distinct \(\sigma_a\ne\sigma_b\) can still carry unitarily equivalent frames — the null algebra never sees the singular values. So the inter-block condition is a genuinely independent hypothesis, not a corner case already handled.

## 4. What failure looks like: the Clebsch–Gordan degeneracy count

The necessity direction comes with an exact degeneracy formula, which is the multi-block analogue of the generating-function count (10j) in the parent paper.

Suppose blocks \(a,b\) are synchronized (\(L_a=L_b=L\), frames unitarily equivalent). Then in the sector \((n_a,n_b)\) the algebra acts as the *same* representation twice, and the vectorized zero space is the commutant of the diagonal \(\mathfrak u(L)\) action on \(\Lambda^{n_a}\mathbb C^L\otimes\Lambda^{n_b}\mathbb C^L\). That tensor product is multiplicity-free with

\[
\dim\ker H(0)\big|_{(n_a,n_b)}
=\min(n_a,\,n_b,\,L-n_a,\,L-n_b)+1 .
\]

The simplest instance: two synchronized blocks with one pair each give a two-dimensional kernel (\(\mathrm{Sym}^2\oplus\Lambda^2\)) where MB3 demands one dimension. These extra states are *interblock dark states* — paired zero modes that are not product AGPs at all. Physically this is the multi-block cousin of the even/odd reducible counterexample of Paper 1's Section 2.2, and a candidate mechanism for stiffness loss that is invisible to the composition-envelope analysis of Section 7: the envelope minimizes over product-AGP branches, but a synchronized model has ground states *outside* that family.

Note the pleasing parallel: disconnection gives the degeneracy count (10j) as a product of geometric-series coefficients; synchronization gives a Littlewood–Richardson (here Pieri-type, multiplicity-free) count. Both failure modes come with exact integer signatures that a numerical audit can confirm, just as the 55-graph audit did in Paper 1.

## 5. Why this looks tractable, and the cheap certificate

Sufficiency is essentially assembled from parts already proved: per-block frame connectivity gives \(\mathfrak u(\mathcal V_a)\) per block by the parent's spanning-tree induction; pairwise inequivalence plus Wedderburn gives fullness of \(\mathcal A\); irreducibility of \(\bigotimes_a\Lambda^{n_a}\mathcal V_a\) under \(\bigoplus_a\mathfrak u(\mathcal V_a)\) (exterior powers of *different* summands) plus Schur gives a scalar commutant, i.e., the unique product AGP. Necessity is the explicit construction of Section 4 above. Neither step needs machinery beyond what Papers 1–2 already use.

The inter-block condition also has a finite, cheap numerical test: \(Wp_\lambda^aW^\dagger=p_\lambda^b\) for rank-one frames means \(Wv_\lambda^a=e^{i\theta_\lambda}v_\lambda^b\) with \(\|v_\lambda^a\|=\|v_\lambda^b\|\) for every \(\lambda\) — a Sylvester-type linear system plus a norm-pattern match. Generic frames fail the norm matching alone, so **generically MB3 is automatic**, and the audit is a few lines of linear algebra per block pair. This belongs in the certificate suite next to the graph audits.

## 6. The genuinely open half: model selection

The representation theory above is the easier half. The substantive open question is exhibiting the class concretely: a QGN Hamiltonian in the Hermitian one-body class whose pairing form has \(q\ge2\) *distinct* nonzero singular values and whose null factors satisfy the blockwise tight-frame condition. The known solvable constructions (uniform-pairing Hubbard, the GHK ideal models) are engineered precisely at equal singular values, where Remark 3.3 merges everything into one block and Paper 1 already applies. The constraint discovered via Proposition 3.1 — frame vectors cannot straddle blocks — significantly narrows where to look, and is itself part of the problem statement: it says the microscopic interaction must resolve the singular blocks channel by channel.

Once a concrete model is in hand, the criterion of Section 3 is checkable on it the same way frame connectivity was checkable on Models I and II — first by audit, then, if the structure cooperates, by an alias-coefficient argument in the style of the two new connectivity proofs.

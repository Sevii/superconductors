# Multi-block reduction law for inequivalent pairing singular values

## Main conclusion

The equal-singular-value reduction theorem does extend, but the correct extension has two layers.

1. **Block-preserving twist.**  After resolving the pair number in every singular-value block, the exact curvature is a sum of independent hard-core factors, one for each pseudospin block, plus a block-Gram longitudinal term.
2. **Generic twist.**  A one-body twist source may transfer a single fermion between singular blocks.  Those terms generate additional universal channels with Clebsch factors
   \[
   \tau_{a\leftarrow b}(\mathbf n)
   =\frac{n_b(L_a-n_a)}{L_a}.
   \]
   In the fixed-total-number zero-mode manifold, the response is then a positive semidefinite matrix on the lattice of block occupations, not a single scalar filling polynomial.

A crucial structural point is that distinct singular values force several commuting pseudospins in the Hermitian one-body QGN class.  Consequently the zero mode is generically **not simple in a fixed total-pair-number sector**.  The clean law is therefore block-sector resolved, or else matrix valued in the degenerate manifold.

## 1. Singular-value blocks force a product pseudospin symmetry

Let the full-rank skew pairing form have skew-Takagi decomposition

\[
F=U\left(\bigoplus_{a=1}^{q}s_aJ_a\right)U^T,
\qquad s_a>0,\quad s_a\neq s_b\ (a\neq b),
\]

where \(J_a^T=-J_a\), \(J_a^\dagger J_a=I\), and \(\dim\mathfrak h_a=2L_a\).  Define

\[
\eta_a^+=\frac12c^\dagger J_ac^\dagger,
\qquad
\eta_a^-=(\eta_a^+)^\dagger,
\qquad
\eta_a^z=\frac{N_a-L_a}{2}.
\]

The \(q\) triples generate commuting copies of \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\).

### Proposition 1 (automatic block splitting)

Let \(S=d\Gamma(s)\) be a Hermitian number-conserving one-body operator.  If

\[
[S,\eta_F^+]=0,
\qquad
\eta_F^+=\frac12c^\dagger Fc^\dagger,
\]

then \(S\) commutes with every block pseudospin:

\[
[S,\eta_a^\pm]=[S,\eta_a^z]=0
\qquad(a=1,\ldots,q).
\]

### Proof

The commutator condition is

\[
sF+Fs^T=0.
\]

Since \(s=s^\dagger\), its adjoint gives

\[
F^\dagger s+s^TF^\dagger=0.
\]

Multiplying the first identity on the right by \(F^\dagger\) and using the second gives

\[
sFF^\dagger=FF^\dagger s.
\]

The eigenspaces of \(FF^\dagger\) are precisely the distinct singular-value blocks, so \(s\) is block diagonal.  On block \(a\),

\[
s_aJ_a+J_as_a^T=0,
\]

which is equivalent to \([d\Gamma(s_a),\eta_a^+]=0\).  Hermiticity gives the lowering commutator, and block diagonality gives the \(\eta_a^z\) commutator.  ∎

### Consequence: total-sector simplicity is impossible

Assume the unperturbed square factors annihilate the vacuum.  Then they annihilate every product AGP

\[
|\mathbf n\rangle
=
\bigotimes_{a=1}^{q}
\left[
\frac{(L_a-n_a)!}{n_a!L_a!}
\right]^{1/2}
(\eta_a^+)^{n_a}|0\rangle,
\qquad 0\le n_a\le L_a.
\]

At fixed total pair number \(n=\sum_an_a\), these states are orthogonal because they carry different block charges.  Hence

\[
\dim\ker H_0\big|_{2n}
\ge
[z^n]\prod_{a=1}^{q}(1+z+\cdots+z^{L_a}).
\]

For \(q>1\), this lower bound exceeds one for every \(1\le n\le \sum_aL_a-1\).  Thus the simple-branch hypothesis used in the one-block theorem cannot hold in the unresolved total-number sector.  One must either fix \(\mathbf n\), add a selector that chooses one branch, or use degenerate perturbation theory.

The weighted AGP generated by the original form is only one vector in this manifold:

\[
(\eta_F^+)^n|0\rangle
\propto
\sum_{|\mathbf n|=n}
\left[
\prod_as_a^{n_a}\sqrt{\binom{L_a}{n_a}}
\right]
|\mathbf n\rangle.
\]

The singular values determine this superposition but do not lift the zero-energy degeneracy.

## 2. Product-pseudospin extension of Lemma 2

Let \(B=d\Gamma(b)\) be any number-conserving one-body operator and write

\[
b=\sum_{a,b}b^{ab},
\qquad b^{ab}=P_abP_b.
\]

For the diagonal blocks define

\[
\beta_a(B)=\frac1{L_a}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathfrak h_a}b^{aa}
=\langle\mathbf e_a|B|\mathbf e_a\rangle,
\]

where \(\mathbf e_a\) denotes one pair in block \(a\) and vacuum in all other blocks.  Define the one-pair seed vectors

\[
|\zeta_a(B)\rangle
=B^{aa}|\mathbf e_a\rangle-\beta_a(B)|\mathbf e_a\rangle,
\]

and, for \(a\neq b\),

\[
|\zeta_{a\leftarrow b}(B)\rangle
=B^{ab}|\mathbf e_b\rangle.
\]

For \(L_a\ge2\), put

\[
\rho_a(n_a)=\frac{n_a(L_a-n_a)}{L_a-1},
\]

and put \(\rho_a=0\) for a one-pair-orbital block \(L_a=1\), where no \(S_a-1\) channel exists.  For ordered distinct blocks define

\[
\tau_{a\leftarrow b}(\mathbf n)
=\frac{n_b(L_a-n_a)}{L_a}.
\]

### Lemma 2B (multi-block one-body action)

There are normalized product-pseudospin ladder isometries \(U_{a,\mathbf n}\) and \(U_{a\leftarrow b,\mathbf n}\), independent of the particular operator \(B\), such that

\[
\boxed{
B|\mathbf n\rangle
=
\left(\sum_an_a\beta_a(B)\right)|\mathbf n\rangle
+
\sum_a\sqrt{\rho_a(n_a)}\,
U_{a,\mathbf n}|\zeta_a(B)\rangle
+
\sum_{a\neq b}\sqrt{\tau_{a\leftarrow b}(\mathbf n)}\,
U_{a\leftarrow b,\mathbf n}|\zeta_{a\leftarrow b}(B)\rangle.
}
\]

The displayed channels are mutually orthogonal.  The first lies in the product of maximal-spin representations; the second lowers block \(a\) from \(S_a\) to \(S_a-1\); the third lowers blocks \(a,b\) to \(S_a-\tfrac12,S_b-\tfrac12\).

### Proof of the new transfer coefficient

The diagonal term is the original one-block Lemma 2 applied in block \(a\) and tensored with all other AGPs.  For an off-diagonal one-body matrix \(x:\mathfrak h_b\to\mathfrak h_a\), let \(B_x=c_a^\dagger xc_b\).  The block AGPs have one-body density matrices

\[
\langle c_a^\dagger c_a\rangle=\frac{n_a}{L_a}I_{2L_a}.
\]

Therefore, for any \(x,y\),

\[
\langle B_y\mathbf n|B_x\mathbf n\rangle
=
\frac{n_b}{L_b}
\left(1-\frac{n_a}{L_a}\right)
\operatorname{Tr}(y^\dagger x).
\]

At the one-pair seed \(|\mathbf e_b\rangle\), the same inner product is

\[
\langle B_y\mathbf e_b|B_x\mathbf e_b\rangle
=
\frac1{L_b}\operatorname{Tr}(y^\dagger x).
\]

The ratio is exactly \(\tau_{a\leftarrow b}(\mathbf n)\).  Polarization proves that the normalized ladder map is an isometry on the complete transfer multiplicity space.  Orthogonality follows from the block-charge weights and the distinct product-spin labels.  ∎

## 3. Exact block-preserving reduction law

Assume now:

1. every unperturbed Hermitian square factor commutes with all block pseudospins;
2. the twist lift preserves every block charge, so each first source \(B_{\lambda i}=d\Gamma(b_{\lambda i})\) is block diagonal;
3. in each fixed block-pair-number sector \(\mathbf n\), the product AGP \(|\mathbf n\rangle\) is a simple gapped zero mode.

Define

\[
\beta_{\lambda i}^{a}
=\frac1{L_a}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathfrak h_a}b_{\lambda i}^{aa},
\]

and the block longitudinal Gram tensor

\[
\Gamma_{ij}^{ab}
=\operatorname{Re}\sum_\lambda
(\beta_{\lambda i}^{a})^*\beta_{\lambda j}^{b}.
\]

For each block let

\[
\Theta_{ij}^{a}
=
\operatorname{Re}
\langle \Pi\mathbf\zeta_i^a,\Pi\mathbf\zeta_j^a\rangle,
\]

where \(\mathbf\zeta_i^a=(\zeta_a(B_{\lambda i}))_\lambda\) and \(\Pi=P_{\ker D^\dagger}\) is the least-squares target projector.  Each \(\Theta^a\) is positive semidefinite.

### Theorem 1B (block-resolved finite-size reduction)

The exact Hessian of the simple branch in sector \(\mathbf n\) is

\[
\boxed{
C_{ij}^{(\mathbf n)}
=
\operatorname{Re}\sum_\lambda
\left(\sum_an_a\beta_{\lambda i}^{a}\right)^*
\left(\sum_bn_b\beta_{\lambda j}^{b}\right)
+
\sum_a\rho_a(n_a)\Theta_{ij}^{a}.
}
\tag{MB1}
\]

Equivalently, since the one-pair block curvature is

\[
C_{ij}^{(\mathbf e_a)}=\Gamma_{ij}^{aa}+\Theta_{ij}^{a},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
C_{ij}^{(\mathbf n)}
=
\sum_a\rho_a(n_a)C_{ij}^{(\mathbf e_a)}
+
\sum_{a,b}n_an_b\Gamma_{ij}^{ab}
-
\sum_a\rho_a(n_a)\Gamma_{ij}^{aa}.
}
\tag{MB2}
\]

### Proof

The direct-sum null map \(D\) intertwines the product group \(\prod_aSU(2)_a\).  Hence \(\Pi\) commutes with every normalized block ladder map and preserves the orthogonal product-spin channels in Lemma 2B.  The longitudinal target vector cannot be removed by a least-squares correction because

\[
D_{\mathbf n}^\dagger
\left(
|\mathbf n\rangle\otimes\mathbf v
\right)
=
\sum_\lambda v_\lambda S_\lambda|\mathbf n\rangle=0.
\]

Projecting the source decomposition and taking channelwise Gram products gives (MB1).  Substitution of the one-pair identity gives (MB2).  ∎

### Important sign point

The first form (MB1) is manifestly positive.  In contrast to the one-block theorem, the correction in (MB2) relative to \(\sum_a\rho_aC^{(\mathbf e_a)}\) need not be positive semidefinite: longitudinal trace sources from different blocks may interfere destructively.  The clean hard-core sum requires the block traces to vanish.

### Trace-free corollary

If

\[
\beta_{\lambda i}^{a}=0
\qquad\text{for all }\lambda,i,a,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
C_{ij}^{(\mathbf n)}
=
\sum_a
\frac{n_a(L_a-n_a)}{L_a-1}
C_{ij}^{(\mathbf e_a)}.
}
\tag{MB3}
\]

This is the exact multi-pseudospin filling law.

At fixed **total** pair number, however, the unperturbed QGN ground space contains every allowed composition.  If the twist preserves block charges, the effective curvature operator is diagonal in the composition basis, so for a real twist direction $v$ the physical directional curvature is

\[
\min_{|\mathbf n|=n} v_i C_{ij}^{(\mathbf n)}v_j.
\]

Thus (MB3) is a branch law.  It becomes a single physical Hessian only after the block filling is fixed, a selector isolates one composition, or the same composition minimizes in a neighborhood of every twist direction.

There is an immediate physical consequence.  In a real direction $v$, suppose the block pair curvatures

\[
\mu_a(v)=v_i(m^{-1}_{\mathrm{pair},a})_{ij}v_j
\]

are nonnegative.  At fixed total pair number, minimizing the trace-free block law is a separable concave minimization problem.  Therefore a minimizing composition has all but at most one block either empty or full.  In particular, whenever

\[
n=\sum_{a\in A}L_a
\]

for some subset $A$ of blocks, the unresolved total-number ground curvature is exactly zero: fill the blocks in $A$ completely and leave the others empty.  In the thermodynamic limit, at fixed pair density $p=n/V$,

\[
D_v^{\mathrm{phys}}(p)
=
\min_{\substack{0\le\nu_a\le1\\ \sum_a\ell_a\nu_a=p}}
\sum_a\ell_a\nu_a(1-\nu_a)\mu_a(v),
\]

and the minimum lies at a vertex of the filling polytope.  Thus the multi-block degeneracy can produce exact commensurate zero-stiffness points even when every partially filled block has positive pair mass.  This is the multi-pseudospin analogue of the paper's reducibility obstruction, not a failure of the block-resolved law.

If the block one-pair branches satisfy

\[
C_{ij}^{(\mathbf e_a)}=4(m^{-1}_{\mathrm{pair},a})_{ij},
\]

then the finite-volume canonical curvature is

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_{ij}^{(\mathbf n)}
=
\frac1V\sum_a
\frac{n_a(L_a-n_a)}{L_a-1}
(m^{-1}_{\mathrm{pair},a})_{ij}.
}
\tag{MB4}
\]

Writing \(\nu_a=n_a/L_a\),

\[
\kappa_{ij}^{(\mathbf n)}
=
\sum_a
\frac{L_a}{V}\frac{L_a}{L_a-1}
\nu_a(1-\nu_a)
(m^{-1}_{\mathrm{pair},a})_{ij}.
\]

Along a regular sequence with \(L_a/V\to\ell_a\), convergent block pair masses, and vanishing blockwise winding traces,

\[
\boxed{
D_{s,ij}
=
\sum_a
\ell_a\nu_a(1-\nu_a)
(m^{-1}_{\mathrm{pair},a})_{ij}.
}
\tag{MB5}
\]

Provided each block-resolved trace symbol obeys the same translation-covariant locality hypothesis, the roots-of-unity/winding argument applies blockwise: fixed finite range kills all \(\beta^a\) exactly beyond the support, while uniform exponential locality suppresses the complete block Gram matrix exponentially. Locality of only the unresolved total trace is not sufficient for this stronger conclusion.

## 4. Generic twist: the exact law is matrix valued

The existing one-block hypotheses only require the source to be one body.  They do **not** force \(b_{\lambda i}^{ab}=0\) for \(a\neq b\).  When such terms are present, fixed block charges cease to define invariant twisted branches and the unavoidable zero-mode degeneracy must be treated directly.

Let

\[
\mathcal Z_n
=
\operatorname{span}
\{|\mathbf n\rangle:\ |\mathbf n|=n\}
\]

and fix a real twist direction \(v\).  Put \(B_{\lambda,v}=v_iB_{\lambda i}\) and define

\[
T_v:\mathcal Z_n\to\ker D^\dagger,
\qquad
T_v|\psi\rangle
=
\Pi(B_{\lambda,v}|\psi\rangle)_\lambda.
\]

The exact second-order effective curvature operator is

\[
\boxed{Q_v^{(n)}=T_v^\dagger T_v\succeq0.}
\tag{MB6}
\]

If the zero manifold is separated from positive energy by a finite-size gap, then

\[
E_0(tv)
=
\frac{t^2}{2}\lambda_{\min}(Q_v^{(n)})+O(t^3).
\]

Thus the physical directional curvature is \(\lambda_{\min}(Q_v^{(n)})\).  A tensor Hessian need not exist if the minimizing eigenvector changes with direction.

For each transfer channel define

\[
k_v^{a\leftarrow b}
=
\|\Pi\mathbf\zeta_v^{a\leftarrow b}\|^2,
\qquad
\omega_v^{ab}
=
\langle
\Pi\mathbf\zeta_v^{b\leftarrow a},
\Pi\mathbf\zeta_v^{a\leftarrow b}
\rangle.
\]

Then the occupation-basis matrix is universal:

\[
\begin{aligned}
(Q_v^{(n)})_{\mathbf n\mathbf n}
={}&
\left\|\sum_an_a\boldsymbol\beta_v^a\right\|^2
+
\sum_a\rho_a(n_a)k_v^a
+
\sum_{a\neq b}\tau_{a\leftarrow b}(\mathbf n)
 k_v^{a\leftarrow b},
\end{aligned}
\tag{MB7}
\]

and its only possible off-diagonal entries connect neighboring compositions.  If

\[
\mathbf m=\mathbf n+\mathbf e_a-\mathbf e_b,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
(Q_v^{(n)})_{\mathbf m\mathbf n}
=
\sqrt{
\frac{
 n_b(L_a-n_a)(n_a+1)(L_b-n_b+1)
}{L_aL_b}}
\,\omega_v^{ab},
}
\tag{MB8}
\]

with the reverse entry fixed by Hermiticity.  All other off-diagonal entries vanish.

The square-root factor is the normalized product-pseudospin matrix element

\[
\frac{
\langle\mathbf n+\mathbf e_a-\mathbf e_b|
\eta_a^+\eta_b^-
|\mathbf n\rangle
}{\sqrt{L_aL_b}}.
\]

Hence the generic multi-block response is a tight-binding problem on the bounded composition lattice, with exactly known Clebsch weights and one-pair channel Gram data as hopping amplitudes.

The usual one-pair inverse masses—the eigenvalues of \(Q_v^{(1)}/4\)—do not by themselves determine \(Q_v^{(n)}\).  The channel-resolved diagonal norms and transfer overlaps are also required.

## 5. Minimal counterexample to a scalar total-filling law

Take two singular blocks with \(L_1=L_2=1\), modes \(a_\pm,b_\pm\), and

\[
S_a=n_{a+}-n_{a-},
\qquad
S_b=n_{b+}-n_{b-}.
\]

Consider the Hermitian positive-square family

\[
H(A)
=
\frac12S_a^2+\frac12S_b^2+\frac12[A B]^2,
\]

with

\[
B=b_+^\dagger a_+ + b_-^\dagger a_-
+a_+^\dagger b_+ + a_-^\dagger b_-.
\]

At two particles the unperturbed zero space is exactly

\[
|a\rangle=a_+^\dagger a_-^\dagger|0\rangle,
\qquad
|b\rangle=b_+^\dagger b_-^\dagger|0\rangle.
\]

Directly,

\[
B|a\rangle=B|b\rangle
=a_+^\dagger b_-^\dagger|0\rangle
-a_-^\dagger b_+^\dagger|0\rangle,
\]

whose norm squared is two.  Therefore

\[
Q^{(1)}=2
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

with eigenvalues \(0,4\).  Each block-resolved diagonal response is positive, but the total-number ground branch has zero curvature because \((|a\rangle-|b\rangle)/\sqrt2\) is annihilated by \(B\).  This rules out any universal scalar law based only on the total filling and positive block pair masses.

## 6. Recommended manuscript statement

A concise replacement for the present “remaining scope” sentence is:

> **Multi-singular-value extension.**  If the skew pairing form has distinct nonzero singular-value blocks of sizes \(2L_a\), Hermitian one-body QGN null factors commute with a product pseudospin group \(\prod_aSU(2)_a\).  The fixed-total-number zero space is therefore generically degenerate and the simple-branch theorem does not extend as a scalar law.  For a block-charge-preserving twist and a simple branch in each fixed block filling \(\mathbf n\), the exact trace-free reduction is
> \[
> C^{(\mathbf n)}=\sum_a\frac{n_a(L_a-n_a)}{L_a-1}C^{(\mathbf e_a)}.
> \]
> With nonzero block traces, the exact replacement is (MB1).  For a generic one-body twist that mixes singular blocks, the second-order response is the positive composition-space operator (MB6)–(MB8), whose only off-diagonal matrix elements transfer one pair between blocks with universal product-pseudospin Clebsch factors.

This closes the algebraic extension of Lemma 2.  What remains model dependent is whether the physical twist preserves the singular blocks, whether a selector fixes their fillings, and whether the channel-resolved one-pair tensors admit simple mass formulas.

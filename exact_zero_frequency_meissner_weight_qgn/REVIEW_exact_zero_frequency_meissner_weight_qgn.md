# Review: "Exact Zero-Frequency Meissner Weight in a Gapless Frustration-Free QGN Parent"

Reviewed 2026-07-27, against the July 26 draft (`exact_zero_frequency_meissner_weight_qgn.tex`), the two verifier scripts, and the retained certificates.

## Summary of the paper

The paper does two things. The general layer proves an exact finite-volume Kubo–Kohn identity (the imaginary-frequency kernel exceeds the Kohn curvature by an explicit positive spectral defect), a sharp necessary-and-sufficient temporal order-of-limits criterion (uniform low-energy H⁻¹ tightness of the current spectral measure), and, for positive-square parents H = D†D/2, an exact Tikhonov/least-squares form of the entire imaginary-frequency response in target space. The model layer introduces a gauge-covariant complete-site-swap parent whose seniority-zero compression is the symmetric-exclusion / ferromagnetic hard-core generator, shows the static subtraction is exactly graph Hodge projection, and concludes that every divergence-free bond field has a frequency- and size-independent response, giving κ_K = D_A = D_M = 2jρ(1−ρ) > 0 with a fixed-number gap closing as L⁻². Two counterexamples show the temporal and transverse obstructions are independent, and a multiblock operator floor is proved.

## Overall assessment

The mathematics is correct as far as I could check, which is essentially everything. Every theorem in the paper was either verified by hand, numerically, or both (details below), and I found no errors in any stated result or proof. The claim discipline is exemplary: the claim hierarchy, CLAIM_STATUS.md, and the non-claims list accurately describe what is and is not proved, and the deliberate separation of the finite-coupling transfer problem is the right call. The two counterexamples are well-chosen and make the modular structure of Theorem 6.2 concrete. This is a strong, publishable-quality draft. My comments are about literature positioning, two definitional gaps, one proof that is written too tersely, and gaps in the verification scripts (one of which I have closed with an independent script included with this review).

## Verification performed

**Re-ran both verifiers.** `verify_dynamical_response.py` and `verify_graph_hodge_meissner.py` both terminate `OVERALL: PASS` (numpy 2.4.4, scipy 1.17.1), and every PASS/VALUE line matches the retained certificates exactly.

**Hand-checked the proofs.** Theorem 3.1 (scalar identity 1/E − E/(E²+ζ²) = ζ²/(E(E²+ζ²)) and Loewner monotonicity); Theorem 4.2 including both scalar inequalities behind the tail bounds (2ζ²/(E(E²+ζ²)) ≥ 1/E for E ≤ ζ; ≤ 2/E below δ; ≤ 2(ζ²/δ²)/E above δ) and both directions of the equivalence; Theorem 5.1 including JP = ½D†S, PTP = S†S, the singular-value identity ½D·Ĥ(Ĥ²+ζ²)⁻¹·D† = 𝓛²/(𝓛²+ζ²), and the Tikhonov normal equation; the factor-2 bookkeeping between state-space and target-space measures in Corollary 5.2; Lemma 7.5 (the α_{N,n} inner product via the zero-mean cancellation); the coefficient algebra in Theorem 7.2 reducing to the multiplier ζ²/((jλ/4)²+ζ²); Theorem 9.1 and γ → 2ρ(1−ρ); the ODLRO eigenvalue n(N−n+1)/N; the gap formula via Aldous (Caputo–Liggett–Richthammer) plus λ₁ = 4sin²(π/L); the cross-term argument in Theorem 10.1; and both counterexamples (the soft-target family reproduces c₀ vs c₀+2α exactly; the free-gas transverse cancellation is standard). All correct.

**Independent full-Fock verification (new).** The paper's own verifiers work entirely in the compressed seniority-zero pair sector, so they do not test the fermionic layer of Sections 7.1–7.2 at all. I wrote an independent implementation (`independent_fock_check.py`, included) that builds the spinful-fermion Fock space with Jordan–Wigner operators on three small graphs (triangle, path P₃, square C₄ with two pairs) and checks, at the full Fock level: that W_e and W_e[A] are Hermitian involutions; that H_G[0] is PSD with a one-dimensional kernel in every even-number sector equal to the Dicke state **with the claimed fermionic signs** and no zero modes in odd sectors; full-Fock gauge covariance U_φ H[A] U_φ† = H[A+B*φ]; that the seniority-zero compression equals (j/4)Σ(1−W_e) with charge-2 Peierls phases e^{∓2iA_e}; and that the full-Fock Kubo response in the fixed-2n sector (with J, T computed as commutators and cross-checked against finite differences of H[ta]) reproduces both the static Hodge formula and the imaginary-frequency graph filter, including exact frequency independence for divergence-free profiles. Everything passes to machine precision. This closes the main verification gap; I would suggest adopting the script (or an equivalent) into the package.

## Major comments

**1. Literature positioning is the weakest part of the draft.** The bibliography covers the order-of-limits line (Kohn, Scalapino–White–Zhang, Resta, Watanabe–Oshikawa, Takasan et al.) and the QGN line (Han et al., Gao et al.), but several directly relevant threads are absent. (a) The quantum-geometry/flat-band superfluid-weight literature — Peotta and Törmä (Nat. Commun. 6, 8944 (2015)) and the subsequent review literature — is the context most readers will bring to a "flat-band stiffness" result, and the paper's exact D_s should be positioned against those bound-type results. (b) Tada and Koma (J. Stat. Phys. 165, 455 (2016), "Two no-go theorems on superconductivity") is specifically about the distinction between orders of limits for the Meissner problem and is arguably the closest antecedent to Section 2; engaging it would sharpen the novelty claim. (c) Since the paper explicitly disclaims "ODLRO alone implies Meissner," it should cite the line of work that asserts a version of that implication under hypotheses — Sewell (J. Stat. Phys. 61, 415 (1990)) and the related Nieh–Su–Zhao work — and say in one sentence how the present unconditional finite-size identity differs. (d) A remark connecting to the η-pairing discussion (states with ODLRO whose superconducting status is subtle) would preempt a predictable referee question, since the Dicke/AGP state is a zero-momentum cousin of that family. Please verify current citation details when adding these; the point is the positioning, not the exact list.

**2. The finite-q transverse kernel is never formally defined.** Equation (12) (eq:DM) invokes 𝒦_{L,T}(q,0) and Definition 6.1 invokes k_{L,T}(q,0) (note also the notational mismatch 𝒦 vs k), but the object is never defined — the profile-dependent kernels 𝒞[a], 𝒦(iζ;a) only appear from Section 7 onward, and only for the graph model. Two things need to be pinned down in Section 2: (i) that 𝒦_{L,T}(q,0) means the static curvature 𝒞_L evaluated on the transverse profile a_{L,q,v} (i.e., ζ→0 taken at finite L), as opposed to the alternative regularization lim_{ζ↓0} lim_{L→∞} 𝒦_{L,T}(q,iζ) at fixed q — these differ in principle, and Theorem 6.2's hypothesis (ii) inherits whichever convention is chosen; and (ii) how q → 0 interacts with the L-dependent set of allowed momenta (e.g., fix q in the reciprocal lattice of a common sublattice and take L along multiples). For the swap model none of this matters — Theorem 9.1 correctly covers all orders because the kernel is ζ- and q-independent at finite size, which is the paper's strongest single statement — but the general definitions should be unambiguous on their own.

**3. Proposition 6.3 (summability) is proved too tersely.** The proof asserts that dominated convergence "permits both the low-energy cutoff and Fourier momentum limits to pass through the thermodynamic limit" in three lines, but the hypothesis is an envelope on a position-space Euclidean correlator while the conclusions are about an operator-valued spectral measure (H⁻¹ tightness) and a q → 0 limit of a lattice Fourier transform; the intermediate steps (time-integral representation of the inverse-energy weight, uniformity of the local thermodynamic limits, treatment of the connected subtraction) deserve either a real half-page proof or an explicit downgrade to a remark with a proof sketch. Since nothing downstream uses it (the model bypasses it, as the paper notes), a downgrade would be costless.

**4. Theorem 10.1's cross-term sentence skips a step.** "The swap and residual sources are orthogonal in target space, so all cross terms vanish" is not by itself sufficient: f_ζ(𝓛) does not preserve the block decomposition in general, since 𝓛 = DD†/2 contains cross-block terms D_sw D_r†. The argument is fine — because (S_sw, 0) lies in ker 𝓛, f_ζ(𝓛) acts on it as the identity, and *then* block orthogonality kills ⟨S_sw ⊕ 0, 0 ⊕ S_r⟩ — and this is in fact the order in which the proof introduces the two facts, but the causal wording should make explicit that the eigenvector invariance is what licenses the orthogonality step. One added clause fixes it.

**5. Proposition 11.2 (clean gas): temperature bookkeeping.** The statement is about a closed-shell ground state (T = 0), but the proof uses n_F with no temperature defined, and the verifier checks the cancellation at finite β = 9 in d = 2. Either state the proof at T = 0 with the step-function occupation (the q → 0 difference quotient concentrating on the Fermi surface), or remark that a smooth occupation is used and the T → 0 limit is uniform. As written there is a small mismatch between statement, proof, and certificate.

**6. Verification-script gaps.** Beyond the missing Fock-level layer (comment above, now covered by the included script), two smaller points: (a) the "one-pair gap at L=3..7" checks use n = 1, where fixed-n exclusion *is* the single-particle walk, so those five checks exercise nothing of the Aldous ingredient; only the 3×3, n = 4 check does. Adding one more multi-pair gap check (e.g., 4×3 torus at half filling, if the dimension is tolerable) would test the actual content. (b) The "two-block selected-composition floor" check verifies a scalar combinatorial inequality about which composition minimizes 2Σj_bα_b — it does not test the operator inequality of Theorem 10.1 (that added positive-square rows cannot lower the floor). A small two-block Fock or compressed-sector model with a nontrivial D_r would test the theorem itself.

## Minor comments

The general kernels 𝒞_L[a], 𝒦_L(iζ; a) for an arbitrary profile could be defined once in Section 2 (currently the uniform-direction case gets eqs. 5–8 and the profile-dependent case first appears in Section 7). — In Section 5, state explicitly that E_L = 0 for positive-square parents so Ĥ_L = H_L(0); it is currently implicit in P_L = P_{ker D_L(0)}. — "Uniformly H⁻¹-tight at zero" is defined and internally consistent, but "uniform H⁻¹ equi-integrability at zero energy" may be closer to standard usage; consider a parenthetical. — A one-line remark that the compressed parent (eq. 45) is exactly the isotropic spin-½ ferromagnet (j/2)Σ_e(¼ − S_x·S_y), with the Peierls twist acting on the XY part with charge 2, would give readers a familiar anchor and make the Matsubara–Matsuda citation concrete; it also makes the L⁻² gap (quadratic magnons) and the protected transverse sector intuitive. — Consider a sentence flagging how non-generic the mechanism is: the transverse *paramagnetic* kernel vanishes identically at finite size (J[a]|Ω⟩ = (ij/2)|Ba⟩ = 0), so D_M is purely diamagnetic and the transverse kernel has no q-dispersion at all; contrasting this with the partial cancellation in a BCS ground state would help readers calibrate what the fine-tuned parent buys. — Related, it may be worth noting that U > 0 enters only through kernel selection and drops out of every response formula. — Gao et al. (2026) lacks a DOI. — The Remark after Theorem 7.2 ("exact graph filter") is excellent and could be promoted more prominently; it is the cleanest statement of how the general H⁻¹ criterion is realized.

## Recommendation

Accept-with-minor-revisions territory, as a standalone exact-parent result. The additions I would prioritize: the literature engagement in comment 1 (especially Tada–Koma and Peotta–Törmä), the definitional fix in comment 2, and folding a Fock-level check into the reproducibility package. None of the comments touches the correctness of the central theorem.

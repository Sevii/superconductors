\title{\textbf{Infrared-Stable Schrieffer--Wolff Transfer of Exact Meissner Weight}\\[0.35em]
\large Target-metric $H_{-1}$ control, an isotropic soft-mode theorem, and the first order-eight microscopic audit}
\author{Nicholas Sledgianowski}
\date{Research note -- July 26, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The exact pair-permutation QGN parent has a positive zero-frequency transverse
Meissner weight even though its fixed-number gap closes quadratically.  The
microscopic multiorbital construction reproduces that parent at order
$\lambda^6\Delta$, with a Peierls-covariant quasi-local remainder of order
$\lambda^8\Delta$.  Local norm control alone does not transfer the response:
a remainder can move the ground branch or couple the transverse current to the
quadratic longitudinal mode, and either effect is amplified by the parent
pseudoinverse.

We isolate a response-safe class and prove the missing comparison theorem for
that class.  If the effective low block is a bounded target-metric deformation
\[
 H_{\eta}(A)=\frac{\eps}{2}D(A)^\dagger
 G_{\eta}(A)D(A),\qquad
 G_{\eta}=\Id+2\eta X,\qquad
 \eps=\lambda^6\Delta,\quad\eta=\lambda^2,
\]
of the exact positive-square parent, then the zero manifold is unchanged and
all static and imaginary-frequency transverse kernels are trapped between the
same parent kernel multiplied by the extremal eigenvalues of $G_\eta$.  The
finite-frequency defect obeys the gap-independent estimate
\[
 0\preceq \cK_\eta(\I\zeta)-\cC_\eta
 \preceq \frac{4\eps\eta^2}{g_-}
 S^\dagger X^\dagger X S,
 \qquad S=(\partial_A D)P,
\]
so the order-$\lambda^8$ response is stable without a uniform many-body gap.
This bound alone is not an exact order-of-limits theorem: a two-dimensional
anisotropic edge metric gives a nonzero $O(\lambda^{10}\Delta)$
Drude--Meissner mismatch.  We therefore prove the sharp spatial refinement.
For a translation-invariant quasi-local row metric whose zero-momentum vector
symbol is point-group scalar, transverse-to-longitudinal mixing is
$O(|q|^2)$, its current $H_{-1}$ weight is $O(\eta^2|q|^2)$, and the Kohn,
Abelian zero-frequency, and static transverse Meissner limits coincide.
Consequently
\[
 D_{\rm micro}=\lambda^6\Delta D_{\rm parent}
 +O(\lambda^8\Delta)>0
\]
for every nonsaturated branch covered by the exact parent theorem.

A local algebraic criterion makes the hypothesis auditable: a cluster term is
state-source safe precisely when it lies in the two-sided parent-row ideal
$r_X=D_X^\dagger X_XD_X$.  We then compute the first connected overlap in the
microscopic Schrieffer--Wolff hierarchy.  Two neighboring bridge controls
produce
\[
 -\frac{\lambda^8\Delta b^4}{2\bar\Delta_m^3}
 \{B_e,B_f\}^2,
\]
and on a three-cell one-pair product-AGP space
$P_{\cZ}\{B_0,B_1\}^2P_{\cZ}=(16/3)\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{psmallmatrix}$.
Thus the bridge-only overlap is not response safe; cancellation by the other
degree-eight clusters or an explicit order-eight counterchannel is genuinely
required.  The infinite-volume problem is thereby reduced to a finite
connected-cluster projection and a point-group symbol audit.  We do not claim
that the unmodified microscopic family has already passed that final audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{The transfer problem after the exact Meissner theorem}

The exact pair-permutation parent has already crossed the conceptual boundary
between a finite-torus flux curvature and a true zero-frequency transverse
matter response.  On a periodic lattice in $d\ge2$, its co-closed current
source lies exactly in $\Ker D^\dagger$.  Every finite-volume transverse kernel
is therefore independent of frequency, while the longitudinal soft mode
cancels the static pure-gauge response.  At fixed density $\rho$ the electronic
link convention gives
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm parent}=2j\rho(1-\rho),
 \label{eq:parent-weight}
\end{equation}
with one quarter of this value in the pair-momentum convention $Q=2A$.  The
same statement survives row stacking with the two-block multiplicative escape
as a positive operator floor.

The microscopic construction is perturbative.  Its Peierls-covariant local
Schrieffer--Wolff low block has the form
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm eff,L}^{[7]}(A)
 =\lambda^6\Delta H_{\rm par,L}(A)+R_L^{(8)}(A),
 \qquad
 \norm{\partial_A^r\cR_L^{(8)}(A)}_\mu
 \le C_{\mu,r}\Delta\abs\lambda^8,
 \quad 0\le r\le2,
 \label{eq:existing-sw}
\end{equation}
where $H_{\rm par}$ is the exact base-plus-multiplicative parent.  The remote
control sector has a fixed positive local gap.  The unresolved issue is the
active remainder: the parent fixed-number gap behaves as $L^{-2}$, so the
operator bound in \eqref{eq:existing-sw} cannot simply be inserted into a
resolvent estimate.

It is useful to separate two possible amplifications.  Write $P$ for the exact
parent zero projector and $Q=1-P$.  A generic perturbation $R$ creates
\begin{equation}
 QRP \quad\text{and}\quad QJ_RP,
 \qquad J_R=(\partial_A R)(0).
 \label{eq:two-sources}
\end{equation}
The first changes the zero-field branch through $H_{\rm par}^+QRP$; the second
changes the current response through $H_{\rm par}^{-1/2}QJ_RP$.  Thus a
current-source estimate alone is not sufficient unless the state source is
known to vanish.  The target-metric class below removes the first problem
exactly and controls the second without a gap.

Set
\begin{equation}
 \eps=\lambda^6\Delta,
 \qquad
 \eta=\lambda^2.
 \label{eq:scales}
\end{equation}
An $O(\lambda^8\Delta)$ correction is then an $O(\eta)$ deformation of the
order-$\eps$ parent.

\section{Target-metric response stability}
\label{sec:metric}

Let $D(t):\cH\to\cT$ be a twice differentiable row map, where $t$ is the
amplitude of a chosen link field.  Put
\begin{equation}
 H_0(t)=\frac12D(t)^\dagger D(t),
 \qquad
 P=P_{\Ker D(0)},
 \qquad
 S=\dot D(0)P.
 \label{eq:parent-row}
\end{equation}
For the exact transverse pair-permutation source,
\begin{equation}
 D^\dagger S=0.
 \label{eq:source-kernel}
\end{equation}
Let $G(t)$ be a positive operator on the target space and define
\begin{equation}
 H_G(t)=\frac{\eps}{2}D(t)^\dagger G(t)D(t).
 \label{eq:metric-parent}
\end{equation}
Since $DP=0$, derivatives of $G(t)$ do not enter the first-order source on the
zero manifold.  If $\widetilde D=G^{1/2}D$, then
$\widetilde S=G^{1/2}S$.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted least-squares response bounds]
\label{thm:metric-bounds}
Assume
\begin{equation}
 0<g_-\Id\preceq G(0)\preceq g_+\Id.
 \label{eq:metric-bounds}
\end{equation}
For every vector in the zero manifold, the static curvature is the weighted
least-squares residual
\begin{equation}
 \langle\psi,\cC_G\psi\rangle
 =\eps\inf_{y\in\cH}
 \langle S\psi-Dy,\,G(S\psi-Dy)\rangle.
 \label{eq:weighted-ls}
\end{equation}
If \eqref{eq:source-kernel} holds, then for every $\zeta>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \eps g_-S^\dagger S
 \preceq \cC_G
 \preceq \cK_G(\I\zeta)
 \preceq \eps g_+S^\dagger S.}
 \label{eq:metric-sandwich}
\end{equation}
These inequalities are independent of the smallest positive singular value of
$D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ordinary positive-square response formula applied to
$\widetilde D=G^{1/2}D$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \cC_G
 =\eps\widetilde S^\dagger P_{\Ker\widetilde D^\dagger}\widetilde S,
 \qquad
 \cK_G(\I\zeta)
 =\eps\widetilde S^\dagger
 \frac{\zeta^2}{\widetilde\cL^2+\zeta^2}
 \widetilde S,
 \quad
 \widetilde\cL=\frac{\eps}{2}\widetilde D\widetilde D^\dagger.
 \label{eq:metric-dynamic}
\end{equation}
The first equality is equivalent to \eqref{eq:weighted-ls}.  Bounding the
weighted norm above and below by $g_\pm$ and using
$S\perp\Ran D$ gives the first and last bounds.  The middle inequality is the
positive spectral defect between the finite-frequency multiplier and its
zero-frequency projector.
\end{proof}

For the microscopic scaling, write
\begin{equation}
 G_\eta=\Id+2\eta X,
 \qquad X=X^\dagger,
 \qquad 2\eta\norm X<1.
 \label{eq:G-eta}
\end{equation}
Then $g_\pm=1\pm2\eta\norm X$ are valid bounds.  The entire transverse kernel
is therefore changed by only $O(\eps\eta)=O(\lambda^8\Delta)$, uniformly in
volume and frequency.  This proves positivity at the precision needed by the
microscopic expansion, but a sharper estimate identifies the exact infrared
risk.

\begin{theorem}[Gap-independent current $H_{-1}$ estimate]
\label{thm:hminusone}
Under \eqref{eq:source-kernel} and \eqref{eq:G-eta},
\begin{equation}
 J_G P
 =\eps\eta D^\dagger XS.
 \label{eq:metric-current}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every $\zeta>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 0\preceq \cK_G(\I\zeta)-\cC_G
 \preceq
 \frac{4\eps\eta^2}{g_-}
 S^\dagger X^\dagger XS.}
 \label{eq:defect-bound}
\end{equation}
If $D^\dagger XS=0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \cK_G(\I\zeta)=\cC_G=\eps S^\dagger G_\eta S
 \quad\text{for every }\zeta>0.
 \label{eq:exact-preservation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:metric-parent} and acting on $P$ gives
$J_GP=(\eps/2)D^\dagger G_\eta S$.  The term with the identity vanishes by
\eqref{eq:source-kernel}, proving \eqref{eq:metric-current}.  The general
spectral representation bounds the positive dynamical defect by twice the
static paramagnetic form:
\begin{equation}
 0\preceq\cK_G(\I\zeta)-\cC_G
 \preceq2PJ_GQH_G^+QJ_GP.
 \label{eq:twice-param}
\end{equation}
Because $H_G\succeq\eps g_-H_0$ on $Q$,
\begin{equation}
 H_G^+\preceq(\eps g_-)^{-1}H_0^+.
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
 DH_0^+D^\dagger=2P_{\Ran D}.
 \label{eq:partial-isometry}
\end{equation}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:twice-param} gives
\eqref{eq:defect-bound}.  If $D^\dagger XS=0$, then $J_GP=0$ and the source
lies in $\Ker\widetilde D^\dagger$, proving \eqref{eq:exact-preservation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform perturbative Meissner floor]
\label{cor:floor}
Suppose the exact parent has transverse kernel density $D_{0,L}$ and
$\sup_LD_{0,L}<\infty$.  A bounded target-metric deformation satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \eps(1-2\eta\norm X)D_{0,L}
 \le D_{G,L}(\zeta,q_T)
 \le\eps(1+2\eta\norm X)D_{0,L}
 \label{eq:floor-bound}
\end{equation}
for every finite $L$, every transverse field in the exact source-kernel class,
and every $\zeta>0$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 D_{G}=\lambda^6\Delta D_0+O(\lambda^8\Delta),
 \label{eq:transfer-order8}
\end{equation}
and the response remains positive for sufficiently small fixed $\lambda$ on
every branch with $D_0>0$.
\end{corollary}

The corollary is already a zero-frequency Meissner transfer to the accuracy of
the microscopic low-block theorem.  It does not yet identify the uniform
Drude limit with the transverse limit at fixed nonzero $\lambda$.  The next
example shows why an extra spatial condition is unavoidable.

\section{A row-relative counterexample to exact order-of-limits transfer}
\label{sec:anisotropic-counterexample}

Consider the gradient row on a two-dimensional torus.  At momentum
$\bq=(q,0)$ its symbol is
\begin{equation}
 d(\bq)=\begin{pmatrix}\e^{\I q}-1\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 t(\bq)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad d(\bq)^\dagger t(\bq)=0.
 \label{eq:edge-symbol}
\end{equation}
Take the bounded anisotropic target metric
\begin{equation}
 G_\eta=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\eta\\2\eta&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<\eta<\frac12.
 \label{eq:anisotropic-G}
\end{equation}
This is an exact row-relative perturbation, preserves the zero state, and
satisfies all volume-uniform norm bounds.  Nevertheless,
\begin{equation}
 T=t^\dagger G_\eta t=1,
 \qquad
 E(\bq)=\frac12d^\dagger G_\eta d=\frac12\abs{\e^{\I q}-1}^2,
 \qquad
 J(\bq)=\frac12d^\dagger G_\eta t=\eta(\e^{-\I q}-1).
 \label{eq:aniso-data}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \cC_\eta(\bq)=1-4\eta^2,
 \qquad
 \cK_\eta(\bq,\I\zeta)
 =1-4\eta^2\frac{E(\bq)^2}{E(\bq)^2+\zeta^2}.
 \label{eq:aniso-kernel}
\end{equation}
At exactly $q=0$ the current source vanishes and the uniform Drude kernel is
one.  Taking $q\to0$ at fixed $\zeta>0$ also gives one, whereas the static
transverse limit is $1-4\eta^2$.  The current $H_{-1}$ weight is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\abs{J(\bq)}^2}{E(\bq)}=2\eta^2,
 \label{eq:aniso-hminusone}
\end{equation}
independent of $q$.  Thus it is bounded but not tight.  In microscopic units
the mismatch is $O(\eps\eta^2)=O(\lambda^{10}\Delta)$: two orders smaller
than the static $O(\lambda^8\Delta)$ uncertainty, but nonzero at fixed
coupling.

By contrast, $G_\eta=(1+2\eta)\Id$ gives $J(\bq)=0$ for every transverse
$t(\bq)$ and preserves all three limits exactly.  The distinction is whether
the zero-momentum metric preserves the spatial vector decomposition.

\section{Point-group isotropy gives uniform transverse $H_{-1}$ tightness}
\label{sec:isotropic-theorem}

We now formulate the sufficient condition relevant to a symmetric
$d$-dimensional microscopic completion.  Let $d(\bq)\in\mathbb C^d$ be the
lattice-gradient symbol and let $t(\bq)\in\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^r$ be a
normalized current-source vector satisfying
\begin{equation}
 (d(\bq)^\dagger\otimes\Id_r)t(\bq)=0.
 \label{eq:transverse-symbol}
\end{equation}
The internal space allows several blocks or several vector-row copies.

\begin{definition}[Hydrodynamically closed geometric row metric]
\label{def:hydro-closed}
A target-metric deformation is hydrodynamically closed if, on the
current-generated soft sector, its response is represented by a positive
matrix symbol $G_\eta(\bq)$ such that
\begin{align}
 E_\eta(\bq)&=\frac{\eps}{2}
 (d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(\bq)(d\otimes\Id),
 \label{eq:hydro-energy}\\
 J_\eta(\bq;t)&=\frac{\eps}{2}
 (d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(\bq)t(\bq),
 \label{eq:hydro-current}\\
 T_\eta(\bq;t)&=\eps t(\bq)^\dagger G_\eta(\bq)t(\bq),
 \label{eq:hydro-stress}
\end{align}
while the orthogonal current-generated sector is uniformly $H_{-1}$-tight.
\end{definition}

This closure is exact for a scalar convolution of the pair-permutation rows.
For a more general SW remainder it is an auditable representation-theoretic
condition on the current-created subspace.

\begin{theorem}[Isotropic transverse infrared theorem]
\label{thm:isotropic}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G_\eta(\bq)=\Id+2\eta X(\bq)$ is uniformly positive and Lipschitz near
$\bq=0$;
\item the deformation is hydrodynamically closed in the sense of
\cref{def:hydro-closed};
\item its zero-momentum vector symbol is point-group scalar,
\begin{equation}
 G_\eta(0)=\Id_d\otimes g_\eta,
 \qquad g_\eta\succ0;
 \label{eq:pointgroup-scalar}
\end{equation}
\item the parent density mode obeys
$E_0(\bq)\asymp\eps\abs{d(\bq)}^2$ near the origin.
\end{enumerate}
Then for every normalized transverse source,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{(d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(\bq)t(\bq)}
 \le C\eta\abs{\bq}^2,
 \label{eq:q2-mixing}
\end{equation}
so its hydrodynamic current $H_{-1}$ weight satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \langle J_\eta,H_\eta^+J_\eta\rangle
 \le C\eps\eta^2\abs{\bq}^2.
 \label{eq:hminusone-q2}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the soft spectral tail obeys
\begin{equation}
 \sup_L\frac1{V_L}
 \langle J_{L,T},H_L^+\mathbf1_{(0,\delta]}(H_L)J_{L,T}\rangle
 \le C\eta^2\delta+o_\delta(1).
 \label{eq:tail-delta}
\end{equation}
It is therefore $H_{-1}$-tight.  The uniform Kohn kernel, the
thermodynamic-first Abelian zero-frequency kernel, and the static transverse
Meissner kernel exist together and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 D_K(t)=D_A(t)=D_M(t)
 =\eps\,t(0)^\dagger(\Id_d\otimes g_\eta)t(0).}
 \label{eq:isotropic-identification}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 D_M=\lambda^6\Delta D_{\rm parent}+O(\lambda^8\Delta).
 \label{eq:isotropic-transfer}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Quasi-locality gives
$\norm{G_\eta(\bq)-G_\eta(0)}\le C\eta\abs{\bq}$.  By
\eqref{eq:transverse-symbol} and \eqref{eq:pointgroup-scalar},
\begin{equation}
 (d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(0)t
 =(d^\dagger\otimes g_\eta)t=0.
\end{equation}
Since $\norm{d(\bq)}=O(\abs\bq)$, the remaining difference gives
\eqref{eq:q2-mixing}.  Dividing the squared current amplitude by the
quadratic energy proves \eqref{eq:hminusone-q2}.  Restricting to energies at
most $\delta$ gives $\eps\abs q^2\lesssim\delta$, hence
\eqref{eq:tail-delta}; the assumed regular sector adds only $o_\delta(1)$.

The static weighted residual in the closed sector is
\begin{equation}
 \cC_\eta(\bq;t)
 =\eps\left[
 t^\dagger G_\eta t
 -t^\dagger G_\eta(d\otimes\Id)
 \bigl((d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(d\otimes\Id)\bigr)^+
 (d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta t
 \right].
 \label{eq:symbol-residual}
\end{equation}
The subtraction is $O(\eps\eta^2\abs q^2)$ and the first term converges to
the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:isotropic-identification}.  At $q=0$ the
gradient vanishes, so the same expression is the Kohn kernel.  The
$H_{-1}$-tightness criterion identifies it with the Abelian zero-frequency
kernel.  This proves all three limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why point-group symmetry is the natural microscopic input]
For the hypercubic point group, the spatial vector representation is
irreducible.  Any zero-momentum row metric commuting with all signed coordinate
permutations is therefore $\Id_d$ on the spatial index, though it may be an
arbitrary positive matrix on block multiplicities.  Equation
\eqref{eq:pointgroup-scalar} is thus forced by a symmetric placement of
identical control gadgets on every oriented bond.  It is not forced in a
one-dimensional construction, where there is no independent transverse
channel.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Microscopic branch floor under an isotropic safe remainder]
\label{cor:micro-floor}
For the $d\ge2$ symmetric extension of the two-block parent, suppose the
order-eight low-block remainder is a hydrodynamically closed geometric row
metric satisfying \eqref{eq:pointgroup-scalar}.  For a selected product-AGP
composition with block densities $\rho_b$, the electronic-link response obeys
\begin{equation}
 D_K=D_A=D_M
 \ge2\lambda^6\Delta
 \left(1-C\lambda^2\right)
 \sum_bj_b\rho_b(1-\rho_b).
 \label{eq:microscopic-floor}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is positive whenever at least one selected block is
nonsaturated and $\lambda$ is sufficiently small.  The pair-momentum
superfluid weight is one quarter of \eqref{eq:microscopic-floor}.
\end{corollary}

\section{A finite-cluster criterion for state-source safety}
\label{sec:row-ideal}

The abstract metric hypothesis can be tested cluster by cluster.  Let $D_X$ be
the stack of all parent rows supported in a finite connected cluster $X$, and
let
\begin{equation}
 p_X=P_{\Ker D_X},
 \qquad q_X=\Id-p_X.
 \label{eq:local-pq}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Two-sided parent-row ideal]
\label{prop:row-ideal}
For a Hermitian cluster operator $r_X$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $p_Xr_X=r_Xp_X=0$;
\item $r_X=q_Xr_Xq_X$;
\item there is a Hermitian target operator $X_X$ such that
\begin{equation}
 r_X=D_X^\dagger X_XD_X.
 \label{eq:local-factorization}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
One explicit choice is
\begin{equation}
 X_X=(D_X^+)^\dagger r_XD_X^+,
 \qquad
 \norm{X_X}\le\norm{D_X^+}^2\norm{r_X}.
 \label{eq:local-X}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence of the first two statements is immediate.  The third implies
the first because $D_Xp_X=0$.  Conversely,
$D_X^+D_X=q_X$, so \eqref{eq:local-X} gives
\begin{equation}
 D_X^\dagger X_XD_X
 =(D_X^+D_X)^\dagger r_X(D_X^+D_X)
 =q_Xr_Xq_X=r_X.
\end{equation}
The norm bound follows by submultiplicativity.
\end{proof}

For the pair-permutation parent on a connected bounded-degree cluster, the
smallest positive eigenvalue in each fixed-pair sector equals the one-particle
graph gap by Aldous' spectral-gap theorem \cite{Caputo2010}.  A standard graph
bound then gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{D_X^+}^2\le C\abs X^2
 \label{eq:local-pseudoinverse}
\end{equation}
(up to fixed onsite and coupling constants).  Polynomial support growth is
absorbed by an exponential interaction weight: for every $0<\mu'<\mu$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_X\e^{\mu'\operatorname{diam}X}\norm{X_X}
 \le C_{\mu-\mu'}
 \sum_X\e^{\mu\operatorname{diam}X}\norm{r_X}.
 \label{eq:locality-loss}
\end{equation}
Thus a quasi-local cluster interaction satisfying the two-sided condition
assembles into a bounded quasi-local target operator.  This is the precise
sense in which a local row-ideal audit controls the zero-field state source
uniformly in volume.

\begin{definition}[Unsafe cluster projection]
Fix the canonical connected-cluster decomposition delivered by the local
Schrieffer--Wolff recursion.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak S_X(r_X)=q_Xr_Xq_X,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak U_X(r_X)=r_X-\mathfrak S_X(r_X).
 \label{eq:safe-unsafe}
\end{equation}
The first term is state-source safe.  The second contains the local ground
compression and the cross terms that can be amplified by the closing parent
pseudoinverse.
\end{definition}

Passing the state-source audit does not by itself prove the isotropic theorem.
One must additionally examine the action of $X_X$ on the current-created
vector representation.  A scalar convolution on row labels passes
immediately; an operator-valued row metric requires a representation audit of
the low-energy source sector.

\section{The first connected order-eight overlap}
\label{sec:overlap}

The microscopic hierarchy assigns bridge amplitude $t_B=\lambda^2b\Delta$.
A connected cluster involving two controls therefore first appears at weighted
degree eight.  Its bridge-only coefficient can be computed independently of
the router details.

\begin{proposition}[Two-control zero-energy Feshbach expansion]
\label{prop:two-control}
Let two equal-gap two-level controls have excitation energy $\Delta_m>0$ and
couple from their joint ground state through bounded Hermitian active operators
$A$ and $B$ with amplitude $t$.  Then the zero-energy Feshbach map on the
control-ground subspace is
\begin{equation}
 F(0)
 =-\frac{t^2}{\Delta_m}(A^2+B^2)
 -\frac{t^4}{2\Delta_m^3}\{A,B\}^2
 +O(t^6).
 \label{eq:two-control-expansion}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the high basis $\ket{10},\ket{01},\ket{11}$, the high block and the
low--high coupling are
\begin{equation}
 H_Q=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \Delta_m&0&tB\\
 0&\Delta_m&tA\\
 tB&tA&2\Delta_m
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C=t\begin{pmatrix}A&B&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:two-control-block}
\end{equation}
Expand $F(0)=-CH_Q^{-1}C^\dagger$.  The quadratic term is immediate.  At
fourth order, a one-control excitation must pass through $\ket{11}$ and return.
The resulting product is
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{t^4}{2\Delta_m^3}
 (AB+BA)(AB+BA),
\end{equation}
which proves \eqref{eq:two-control-expansion}.
\end{proof}

For neighboring microscopic bridge operators $B_e,B_f$, this contributes
\begin{equation}
 R_{ef,\rm bridge}^{(8)}
 =-\lambda^8\Delta\frac{b^4}{2\bar\Delta_m^3}
 \{B_e,B_f\}^2.
 \label{eq:bridge-overlap}
\end{equation}
This is only one member of the complete degree-eight cluster sum; router,
shell, screening, Kramers-partner, and folded terms must be added before
judging the full coefficient.

The bridge-only term nevertheless fails the local row-ideal test.  On a
three-cell ring in the two-electron sector, let $P_{\cZ_1}$ project onto the
one-pair product-AGP basis $\ket{1,0},\ket{0,1}$.  Direct fermionic action gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 P_{\cZ_1}\{B_0,B_1\}^2P_{\cZ_1}
 =\frac{16}{3}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.}
 \label{eq:unsafe-compression}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\mathfrak U(\{B_0,B_1\}^2)\ne0$.  The retained verification
script constructs the fixed two-electron basis with exact fermionic signs and
also finds
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\{B_0,B_1\}^2P_{\cZ_1}}=8\sqrt2.
 \label{eq:unsafe-source-norm}
\end{equation}
The result is not a counterexample to the complete microscopic model, because
other degree-eight coefficients may cancel \eqref{eq:unsafe-compression}.  It
is a proof that such a cancellation is a real theorem obligation rather than
a consequence of locality or Peierls covariance.

\section{A response-safe order-eight completion}
\label{sec:completion}

The preceding results suggest a constructive completion of the finite-order
microscopic hierarchy.  Let
\begin{equation}
 \cR^{(8)}=\sum_Xr_X^{(8)}
 \label{eq:R8-clusters}
\end{equation}
be its canonical connected-cluster coefficient.  First remove the unsafe
piece
\begin{equation}
 \cR_{\rm unsafe}^{(8)}
 =\sum_X\mathfrak U_X(r_X^{(8)}).
 \label{eq:unsafe-sum}
\end{equation}
Then replace the safe target operator on the current-generated vector sector
by its point-group average.  Denote the resulting geometric row-metric
interaction by $\cR_{\rm geom}^{(8)}(A)$, lifted with the fully Peierls-covariant
rows $D_X(A)$.

\begin{theorem}[Order-eight response-safe completion]
\label{thm:completion}
Assume the $d\ge2$ microscopic gadget array is translation invariant and
closed under the lattice point group.  There is a Peierls-covariant quasi-local
active counterinteraction $\cC^{(8)}(A)$ with
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\partial_A^r\cC^{(8)}(A)}_{\mu'}
 \le C_{\mu',r}\Delta\lambda^8,
 \qquad 0\le r\le2,
 \label{eq:counterterm-bound}
\end{equation}
such that the order-eight low-block coefficient becomes
$\cR_{\rm geom}^{(8)}(A)$.  Consequently, the seventh-order/degree-eight
completed low block satisfies the isotropic infrared theorem and
\begin{equation}
 D_K^{[8]}=D_A^{[8]}=D_M^{[8]}
 =\lambda^6\Delta D_{\rm parent}+O(\lambda^8\Delta).
 \label{eq:completed-transfer}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At the effective-interaction level, take
\begin{equation}
 \cC^{(8)}_{\rm eff}
 =\cR_{\rm geom}^{(8)}-\cR^{(8)}.
 \label{eq:eff-counterterm}
\end{equation}
The row-ideal factorization and \eqref{eq:locality-loss} give the required
quasi-locality and derivative bounds for the safe part; the unsafe part is
already quasi-local by the SW cluster estimate.  Point-group averaging is a
norm-nonincreasing finite sum and preserves Peierls covariance.

An active microscopic counterinteraction inserted with coefficient
$\lambda^8\Delta$ is acted on by the fixed degree-seven SW unitary as the
identity plus $O(\lambda)$ in local interaction norm.  Therefore its degree-eight
low-block coefficient is exactly itself; all conjugation corrections begin at
degree nine.  Choosing the microscopic coefficient
\eqref{eq:eff-counterterm} proves the order-eight statement.  The resulting
row metric satisfies \cref{thm:isotropic}, giving
\eqref{eq:completed-transfer}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the completion]
The theorem allows a general quasi-local active counterinteraction.  It does
not prove that the counterterm can be synthesized using only the particular
exchange/correlated-hopping tensor already present in the microscopic paper.
A recursive completion can be performed coefficient by coefficient to any
fixed weighted order.  An exact finite-$\lambda$ theorem would additionally
need convergence, or a nonperturbative local Feshbach map with the same
response-safe range.
\end{remark}

\section{What is now reduced to finite algebra}
\label{sec:program}

For the unmodified microscopic family, the next calculation is no longer an
open-ended thermodynamic resolvent estimate.  It is the following finite audit.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Enumerate every connected weighted-degree-eight cluster in the local SW
recursion, including two-control bridge paths, router insertions, shell and
screening vertices, folded terms, and the Kramers closed-shell partner.
\item Compress each canonical coefficient to the local parent kernel and
compute $\mathfrak U_X(r_X^{(8)})$.  Translation and particle-hole symmetries
reduce the number of independent clusters.
\item Sum symmetry-related clusters.  If the unsafe sum vanishes, factor the
remainder with \eqref{eq:local-X}.  If it does not, add the minimal local
counterchannel that cancels it.
\item In the $d\ge2$ extension, compute the zero-momentum symbol of the safe
row metric on the current-created vector representation.  A point-group
complete gadget arrangement should force the spatial factor to be $\Id_d$;
this must be checked after all internal row multiplicities are included.
\item Verify the finite-cluster identities exactly and then use
\cref{thm:isotropic} to obtain the uniform transverse $H_{-1}$ bound.  No
large-volume diagonalization is required.
\end{enumerate}

The theorem boundary can be summarized as follows.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}

The principal correction to the earlier roadmap is that ``control the
remainder current in $H_{-1}$'' is not, by itself, the complete finite-coupling
statement.  A generic remainder also creates a zero-field state source.  The
two-sided row ideal removes that source exactly.  Within this class, the
closing parent gap is no longer dangerous at the order needed for the
microscopic expansion: the response changes by $O(\lambda^8\Delta)$, and the
entire finite-frequency defect is only $O(\lambda^{10}\Delta)$.

The anisotropic edge example then identifies the final sharp distinction.
Bounded $H_{-1}$ weight is not the same as tight $H_{-1}$ weight.  A local
row-metric perturbation can rotate a transverse source into the quadratic
longitudinal mode with amplitude proportional to $q$, leaving a constant
$E^{-1}$ weight.  Point-group scalar zero-momentum structure supplies the
missing power of $q$.  It turns the mixing amplitude into $O(q^2)$ and makes
the soft tail vanish.

For a response-safe, point-group-complete $d\ge2$ microscopic extension, this
proves the desired transfer:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 D_K=D_A=D_M
 =\lambda^6\Delta D_{\rm parent}+O(\lambda^8\Delta)>0.}
 \label{eq:final-box}
\end{equation}
The result is uniform in the transverse infrared window and does not assume a
uniform many-body gap.

The unmodified multiorbital model has not yet been shown to belong to this
class.  The first degree-eight audit finds a concrete unsafe bridge-overlap
term.  That finding is useful rather than merely negative: it reduces the
remaining research to an explicit finite list of connected clusters and
identifies the counterchannel that must emerge or be added.  The next paper can
therefore be organized around a complete order-eight cluster certificate,
followed by the point-group symbol audit and, finally, an all-orders or
nonperturbative block-map theorem.

\section{State-space derivation of the target-metric formulas}

Let $\widetilde H=(\eps/2)\widetilde D^\dagger\widetilde D$ with

 JP=\frac\eps2\widetilde D^\dagger\widetilde S,

 TP=\eps\widetilde S^\dagger\widetilde S,
 \qquad
 \widetilde S=G^{1/2}S.
\end{equation}
The imaginary-frequency Kubo kernel is
\begin{equation}
 \cK(\I\zeta)
 =\eps\widetilde S^\dagger\widetilde S
 -2PJQ\widetilde H(\widetilde H^2+\zeta^2)^{-1}QJP.
 \label{eq:state-kubo-app}
\end{equation}
Using the singular-value decomposition of $\widetilde D$ reduces
\eqref{eq:state-kubo-app} to \eqref{eq:metric-dynamic}.  At $\zeta=0$, the

The defect bound follows from
\begin{equation}
 0\le\frac{\zeta^2}{E(E^2+\zeta^2)}\le\frac1E.
\end{equation}

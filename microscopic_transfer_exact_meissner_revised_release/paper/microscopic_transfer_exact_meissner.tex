\title{\textbf{Microscopic Transfer of Exact Meissner Weight}\\[0.35em]
\large Infrared-stable Schrieffer--Wolff theory, a complete degree-eight no-go,\\
and an exact dark-branch finite-coupling completion}
\author{Nicholas Sledgianowski}
\date{Research manuscript -- July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The exact pair-permutation QGN parent has a positive zero-frequency transverse
Meissner weight although its fixed-number gap closes as $L^{-2}$.  A screened
multiorbital construction reproduces that parent at order $\lambda^6\Delta$
but leaves a Peierls-covariant quasi-local remainder at order
$\lambda^8\Delta$.  We resolve the resulting microscopic-transfer problem in
three steps.  First, target-metric deformations admit finite-volume static and
imaginary-frequency response bounds independent of the closing parent gap, and
their current defect is controlled in the $H_{-1}$ topology at order
$\lambda^{10}\Delta$.  A bounded anisotropic metric nevertheless produces a
finite Drude--Meissner mismatch, showing that row-relative form alone is not
enough; hydrodynamic closure together with point-group scalar zero-momentum
structure restores uniform $H_{-1}$ tightness and yields $D_K=D_A=D_M$.
Second, a complete weighted-degree-eight resolvent audit gives a closed source
formula on the product-AGP manifold.  A two-pair witness proves that the
unmodified matched single-flavor and closed-shell Kramers families cannot
preserve the complete composition manifold at that order.  An explicit
finite-range higher-body counterterm cancels the full unsafe source and restores
the two-sided row ideal through degree eight, with response transfer conditional
on the stated hydrodynamic audit.  Third, the primitive bridge has an exact
coherent dark channel
$\ket{\Omega_n^-}\propto(\eta_1^+-\eta_2^+)^n\ket0$.  Strict local
Schur-complement overcompensation makes it the unique fixed-number ground state
of a positive-semidefinite finite-coupling family.  In a point-group-complete
$d\ge2$ Peierls lift, the swap backbone yields the rigorous transverse floor
$D_*=2\lambda^6\Delta(j_1+j_2)\rho(1-\rho)>0$.  This is a finite-coupling
zero-frequency microscopic Meissner lower bound for a selected coherent branch,
not an all-composition transfer of the original matched family or an equality
theorem for every gadget contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}
\label{sec:intro}

For a selected finite-volume ground branch, let $\cC_{L,T}(q)$ be the static
curvature under a transverse Peierls field and let
$\cK_{L,T}(q,\I\zeta)$ be the corresponding imaginary-frequency matter
kernel.  The three quantities relevant here are, schematically,
\begin{align}
 D_K&=\lim_{L\to\infty}V_L^{-1}\cC_L(q=0),
 \label{eq:DK-def}\\
 D_A&=\lim_{\zeta\downarrow0}\lim_{L\to\infty}
 V_L^{-1}\cK_L(q=0,\I\zeta),
 \label{eq:DA-def}\\
 D_M&=\lim_{\substack{q\to0\\q\cdot t=0}}\lim_{L\to\infty}
 V_L^{-1}\cC_{L,T}(q).
 \label{eq:DM-def}
\end{align}
Kohn curvature, the Abelian zero-frequency uniform kernel, and the transverse
Meissner kernel are distinct in general \cite{Kohn1964,Scalapino1993}.  The
current-spectral $H_{-1}$ criterion identifying the first two limits is developed
in \cite{SledgianowskiDynamic}.  The exact pair-permutation parent is
exceptional \cite{SledgianowskiMeissner}: its transverse source lies in the
target-space cokernel, so the finite-volume kernel is independent of both
$q$ and $\zeta$ on every co-closed field.  At density $\rho$ its electronic-link
weight is
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm parent}=2j\rho(1-\rho),
 \label{eq:parent-weight}
\end{equation}
with one quarter of this value in the pair-phase convention $Q=2A$.

The microscopic construction is perturbative.  A local
Schrieffer--Wolff block diagonalization \cite{Bravyi2011,SledgianowskiMicro}
gives
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm eff,L}^{[7]}(A)
 =\lambda^6\Delta H_{\rm par,L}(A)+R_L^{(8)}(A),
 \qquad
 \norm{\partial_A^rR_L^{(8)}(A)}_\mu
 \le C_{\mu,r}\Delta\abs\lambda^8,
 \quad 0\le r\le2.
 \label{eq:existing-sw}
\end{equation}
Here $\norm{\Phi}_\mu:=\sup_x\sum_{X\ni x}
\e^{\mu\operatorname{diam}X}\norm{\Phi_X}$ denotes the exponentially weighted
quasi-local interaction norm for the interaction $\Phi=\{\Phi_X\}$; harmless
fixed support-size factors may be absorbed into its constants.  The parent gap
closes quadratically, so the local norm estimate does not control the response
by itself.  Writing $P$ for the exact parent zero projector and
$Q=1-P$, a generic remainder creates two infrared-sensitive vectors,
\begin{equation}
 Q R^{(8)}P,
 \qquad
 Q\bigl(\partial_A R^{(8)}\bigr)P.
 \label{eq:two-sources}
\end{equation}
The first moves the zero-field branch; the second changes the current source.
The paper resolves these two issues in three stages.

\paragraph{Part I: a sharp transfer framework.}
A two-sided parent-row factorization removes the state source exactly.  The
remaining current source can be controlled without a spectral gap by passing to
target space.  The resulting theorem gives a uniform perturbative floor, but an
explicit anisotropic metric proves that bounded $H_{-1}$ weight is not enough
for exact temporal/transverse identification.  Point-group isotropy supplies
the missing power of momentum and yields $D_K=D_A=D_M$.

\paragraph{Part II: a complete degree-eight decision.}
The full canonical degree-eight coefficient of the unmodified screened family
is enumerated and compressed to the complete product-AGP manifold.  The
resulting two-pair witness proves that all-composition transfer fails.  This is
stronger than the order-eight overlap remainder left unclassified in the
microscopic construction \cite{SledgianowskiMicro}, because every single-flavor
class and the closed-shell Kramers correction are included.  The same formula
produces the exact finite-range counterterm needed to restore the Part I
state-source hypothesis through degree eight.  The current-sector closure and
zero-momentum symbol remain a separate, finite audit.

\paragraph{Part III: a nonperturbative selected-branch route.}
The crossed bridge itself annihilates one coherent interblock AGP.  Strict local
overcompensation turns that branch into the exact ground state of a positive
finite-coupling microscopic Hamiltonian.  A direct-sum row argument then shows
that no gadget relaxation can cancel the transverse swap-backbone source,
which gives a positive microscopic Meissner floor before any small-gap
perturbation theory is invoked.

The final claim boundary is therefore sharp.  The unmodified matched family
fails to preserve its complete composition manifold at weighted degree eight.
A finite-order all-composition repair exists but is not yet synthesized from
restricted two-electron channels or resummed to all orders.  A modified
positive family already has an exact finite-coupling dark ground branch and a
rigorous zero-frequency transverse floor.  The Part I theorem states the
additional regularity needed to promote that floor to equality of the total
Kohn, Abelian, and Meissner kernels.

\part{Infrared-stable transfer framework}

\section{Target-metric response stability}
\label{sec:metric}

Let $D(t):\cH\to\cT$ be a twice differentiable row map, where $t$ is the
amplitude of a chosen link field.  Put
\begin{equation}
 H_0(t)=\frac12D(t)^\dagger D(t),
 \qquad
 P=P_{\Ker D(0)},
 \qquad
 S=\dot D(0)P.
 \label{eq:parent-row}
\end{equation}
For the exact transverse pair-permutation source,
\begin{equation}
 D^\dagger S=0.
 \label{eq:source-kernel}
\end{equation}
Let $G(t)$ be a positive operator on the target space and define
\begin{equation}
 H_G(t)=\frac{\eps}{2}D(t)^\dagger G(t)D(t).
 \label{eq:metric-parent}
\end{equation}
Since $DP=0$, derivatives of $G(t)$ do not enter the first-order source on the
zero manifold.  If $\widetilde D=G^{1/2}D$, then
$\widetilde S=G^{1/2}S$.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted least-squares response bounds]
\label{thm:metric-bounds}
Assume
\begin{equation}
 0<g_-\Id\preceq G(0)\preceq g_+\Id.
 \label{eq:metric-bounds}
\end{equation}
For every vector in the zero manifold, the static curvature is the weighted
least-squares residual
\begin{equation}
 \langle\psi,\cC_G\psi\rangle
 =\eps\inf_{y\in\cH}
 \langle S\psi-Dy,\,G(S\psi-Dy)\rangle.
 \label{eq:weighted-ls}
\end{equation}
If \eqref{eq:source-kernel} holds, then for every $\zeta>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \eps g_-S^\dagger S
 \preceq \cC_G
 \preceq \cK_G(\I\zeta)
 \preceq \eps g_+S^\dagger S.}
 \label{eq:metric-sandwich}
\end{equation}
These inequalities are independent of the smallest positive singular value of
$D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ordinary positive-square response formula applied to
$\widetilde D=G^{1/2}D$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \cC_G
 =\eps\widetilde S^\dagger P_{\Ker\widetilde D^\dagger}\widetilde S,
 \qquad
 \cK_G(\I\zeta)
 =\eps\widetilde S^\dagger
 \frac{\zeta^2}{\widetilde\cL^2+\zeta^2}
 \widetilde S,
 \quad
 \widetilde\cL=\frac{\eps}{2}\widetilde D\widetilde D^\dagger.
 \label{eq:metric-dynamic}
\end{equation}
The first equality is equivalent to \eqref{eq:weighted-ls}.  Bounding the
weighted norm above and below by $g_\pm$ and using
$S\perp\Ran D$ gives the first and last bounds.  The middle inequality is the
positive spectral defect between the finite-frequency multiplier and its
zero-frequency projector.
\end{proof}

For the microscopic scaling, set $\eps=\lambda^6\Delta$ and
$\eta=\lambda^2$, and write
\begin{equation}
 G_\eta=\Id+2\eta X,
 \qquad X=X^\dagger,
 \qquad 2\eta\norm X<1.
 \label{eq:G-eta}
\end{equation}
Then $g_\pm=1\pm2\eta\norm X$ are valid bounds.  The entire transverse kernel
is therefore changed by only $O(\eps\eta)=O(\lambda^8\Delta)$, uniformly in
volume and frequency.  This proves positivity at the precision needed by the
microscopic expansion, but a sharper estimate identifies the exact infrared
risk.

\begin{theorem}[Gap-independent current $H_{-1}$ estimate]
\label{thm:hminusone}
Under \eqref{eq:source-kernel} and \eqref{eq:G-eta},
\begin{equation}
 J_G P
 =\eps\eta D^\dagger XS.
 \label{eq:metric-current}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every $\zeta>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 0\preceq \cK_G(\I\zeta)-\cC_G
 \preceq
 \frac{4\eps\eta^2}{g_-}
 S^\dagger X^\dagger XS.}
 \label{eq:defect-bound}
\end{equation}
If $D^\dagger XS=0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \cK_G(\I\zeta)=\cC_G=\eps S^\dagger G_\eta S
 \quad\text{for every }\zeta>0.
 \label{eq:exact-preservation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:metric-parent} and acting on $P$ gives
$J_GP=(\eps/2)D^\dagger G_\eta S$.  The term with the identity vanishes by
\eqref{eq:source-kernel}, proving \eqref{eq:metric-current}.  The general
spectral representation bounds the positive dynamical defect by twice the
static paramagnetic form:
\begin{equation}
 0\preceq\cK_G(\I\zeta)-\cC_G
 \preceq2PJ_GQH_G^+QJ_GP.
 \label{eq:twice-param}
\end{equation}
Because $H_G\succeq\eps g_-H_0$ on $Q$,
\begin{equation}
 H_G^+\preceq(\eps g_-)^{-1}H_0^+.
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
 DH_0^+D^\dagger=2P_{\Ran D}.
 \label{eq:partial-isometry}
\end{equation}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:twice-param} gives
\eqref{eq:defect-bound}.  If $D^\dagger XS=0$, then $J_GP=0$ and the source
lies in $\Ker\widetilde D^\dagger$, proving \eqref{eq:exact-preservation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform perturbative transverse floor]
\label{cor:floor}
Let $D_{G,L}(\zeta,q_T)$ denote the volume-normalized kernel obtained from
$\cK_G(q_T,\I\zeta)$ on a normalized allowed lattice-transverse source of
momentum $q_T$, and let $D_{0,L}$ be the corresponding exact-parent density.
Suppose $\sup_LD_{0,L}<\infty$.  A bounded target-metric deformation satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \eps(1-2\eta\norm X)D_{0,L}
 \le D_{G,L}(\zeta,q_T)
 \le\eps(1+2\eta\norm X)D_{0,L}
 \label{eq:floor-bound}
\end{equation}
for every finite $L$, every transverse field in the exact source-kernel class,
and every $\zeta>0$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 D_{G}=\lambda^6\Delta D_0+O(\lambda^8\Delta),
 \label{eq:transfer-order8}
\end{equation}
and the response remains positive for sufficiently small fixed $\lambda$ on
every branch with $D_0>0$.
\end{corollary}

The corollary gives a uniform positive transverse floor for a truncated
response-safe low block.  It does not, by itself, identify the exactly uniform
Kohn/Drude limit with the transverse $q\to0$ limit at fixed nonzero
$\lambda$.  The next example shows why an extra spatial condition is
unavoidable.

\section{A row-relative counterexample to exact order-of-limits transfer}
\label{sec:anisotropic-counterexample}

Consider the gradient row on a two-dimensional torus.  At momentum
$\bq=(q,0)$ its symbol is
\begin{equation}
 d(\bq)=\begin{pmatrix}\e^{\I q}-1\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 t(\bq)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad d(\bq)^\dagger t(\bq)=0.
 \label{eq:edge-symbol}
\end{equation}
Take the bounded anisotropic target metric
\begin{equation}
 G_\eta=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\eta\\2\eta&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<\eta<\frac12.
 \label{eq:anisotropic-G}
\end{equation}
This is an exact row-relative perturbation, preserves the zero state, and
satisfies all volume-uniform norm bounds.  Nevertheless,
\begin{equation}
 T=t^\dagger G_\eta t=1,
 \qquad
 E(\bq)=\frac12d^\dagger G_\eta d=\frac12\abs{\e^{\I q}-1}^2,
 \qquad
 J(\bq)=\frac12d^\dagger G_\eta t=\eta(\e^{-\I q}-1).
 \label{eq:aniso-data}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \cC_\eta(\bq)=1-4\eta^2,
 \qquad
 \cK_\eta(\bq,\I\zeta)
 =1-4\eta^2\frac{E(\bq)^2}{E(\bq)^2+\zeta^2}.
 \label{eq:aniso-kernel}
\end{equation}
At exactly $q=0$ the current source vanishes and the uniform Drude kernel is
one.  Taking $q\to0$ at fixed $\zeta>0$ also gives one, whereas the static
transverse limit is $1-4\eta^2$.  The current $H_{-1}$ weight is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\abs{J(\bq)}^2}{E(\bq)}=2\eta^2,
 \label{eq:aniso-hminusone}
\end{equation}
independent of $q$.  Thus it is bounded but not tight.  In microscopic units
the mismatch is $O(\eps\eta^2)=O(\lambda^{10}\Delta)$: two orders smaller
than the static $O(\lambda^8\Delta)$ uncertainty, but nonzero at fixed
coupling.

By contrast, $G_\eta=(1+2\eta)\Id$ gives $J(\bq)=0$ for every transverse
$t(\bq)$ and preserves all three limits exactly.  The distinction is whether
the zero-momentum metric preserves the spatial vector decomposition.

\section{Point-group isotropy gives uniform transverse \texorpdfstring{$H_{-1}$}{H-minus-one} tightness}
\label{sec:isotropic-theorem}

We now formulate the sufficient condition relevant to a symmetric
$d$-dimensional microscopic completion.  Let $d(\bq)\in\mathbb C^d$ be the
lattice-gradient symbol and let $t(\bq)\in\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^r$ be a
normalized current-source vector satisfying
\begin{equation}
 (d(\bq)^\dagger\otimes\Id_r)t(\bq)=0.
 \label{eq:transverse-symbol}
\end{equation}
The internal space allows several blocks or several vector-row copies.

\begin{definition}[Hydrodynamically closed geometric row metric]
\label{def:hydro-closed}
Fix a selected ground branch and a sufficiently small nonzero lattice momentum
$\bq$.  In the translation-$\bq$ fiber define the current-cyclic subspace
\begin{equation}
 \cH_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\bq)
 =\overline{\Span}\left\{
 p(H_\eta(\bq))QJ_T(\bq)P\xi:
 p\in\mathbb C[z],\ \xi\in P\cH
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:current-cyclic}
\end{equation}
Call an orthogonal decomposition
$\cH_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\bq)=\cH_{\mathrm{soft}}(\bq)\oplus
\cH_{\mathrm{reg}}(\bq)$ admissible if both summands are invariant under
$H_\eta(\bq)$.  Let $D_{\mathrm{reg},L}(\bq,\I\zeta;t)$ denote the complete
kernel-density contribution left after subtracting the soft-sector response
below, including any residual stress term.  We require this regular contribution
to be uniformly $H_{-1}$-tight, to possess a thermodynamic limit jointly
continuous at $(\bq,\zeta)=(0,0)$, and to obey
\begin{equation}
 \abs{D_{\mathrm{reg}}(\bq,\I\zeta;t)}
 \le C\eps\eta\norm{t}^2
 \label{eq:regular-bound}
\end{equation}
near the origin.  A target-metric deformation is \emph{hydrodynamically
closed} if there is such an admissible decomposition, a momentum-intertwining
unitary
\begin{equation}
 U_{\bq}:\cH_{\mathrm{soft}}(\bq)\longrightarrow\mathbb C^r
 \label{eq:closure-intertwiner}
\end{equation}
and a positive matrix symbol $G_\eta(\bq)$ such that, writing
$\Pi_{\mathrm{soft}}(\bq)$ for the soft projection, the compressed Hamiltonian,
current source, and associated stress are exactly
\begin{align}
 U_{\bq}H_\eta(\bq)\Pi_{\mathrm{soft}}(\bq)U_{\bq}^\dagger
 &=E_\eta(\bq)
 =\frac{\eps}{2}(d^\dagger\otimes\Id)
 G_\eta(\bq)(d\otimes\Id),
 \label{eq:hydro-energy}\\
 U_{\bq}\Pi_{\mathrm{soft}}(\bq)QJ_T(\bq)P
 &=J_\eta(\bq;t)
 =\frac{\eps}{2}(d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(\bq)t(\bq),
 \label{eq:hydro-current}\\
 T_\eta(\bq;t)
 &=\eps t(\bq)^\dagger G_\eta(\bq)t(\bq).
 \label{eq:hydro-stress}
\end{align}
Thus the full selected-branch kernel density is the sum of the exact soft
metric response determined by \eqref{eq:hydro-energy}--\eqref{eq:hydro-stress}
and $D_{\mathrm{reg},L}$.  This formulation makes ``represented by a symbol''
an explicit momentum-fiber intertwining statement rather than a heuristic
identification.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Exact scalar-convolution closure]
\label{ex:scalar-convolution}
Let the parent rows be the lattice gradient tensored with an internal row space,
and let the target metric be a translation-invariant absolutely summable
convolution,
\begin{equation}
 (G\psi)_{i,\alpha}(x)
 =\sum_{y,j,\beta}g_{i\alpha,j\beta}(x-y)\psi_{j,\beta}(y).
 \label{eq:convolution-metric}
\end{equation}
The Bloch transform is the intertwiner in
\eqref{eq:closure-intertwiner} and diagonalizes the row metric with symbol
$G(\bq)=\sum_x\e^{-\I\bq\cdot x}g(x)$.  On the density-generated soft fiber
$d(\bq)\otimes\mathbb C^r$, the three compressed operators are exactly
\eqref{eq:hydro-energy}--\eqref{eq:hydro-stress}.  Thus a scalar convolution of
the pair-permutation rows is not merely an analogy: it satisfies the closure
definition operator-theoretically.  Here
$\cH_{\mathrm{reg}}(\bq)=\{0\}$ and $D_{\mathrm{reg}}=0$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Finite audit required for an operator-valued SW remainder]
\label{rem:closure-audit}
For a completed microscopic $d\ge2$ lift, the closure hypothesis is discharged
by a finite sequence of checks: construct the translation-$\bq$ cyclic subspace
of $QJ_T(\bq)P$; verify that all modes with energy $O(\abs\bq^2)$ are invariant
under the low block; compute the compressed $H$, $J$, and $T$ matrices and
identify a common symbol $G_\eta(\bq)$; prove uniform $H_{-1}$ tightness on the
remaining current-generated sector; and check that $G_\eta(0)$ commutes with the
full point group.  These checks have not been completed for the full
operator-valued degree-eight current sector of the explicit $\cC_8$ lift.
Consequently, the truncated equality claimed later remains conditional on this
audit rather than following from row-relative form alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Isotropic transverse infrared theorem]
\label{thm:isotropic}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G_\eta(\bq)=\Id+2\eta X(\bq)$ is uniformly positive and Lipschitz near
$\bq=0$;
\item the deformation is hydrodynamically closed in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:hydro-closed};
\item its zero-momentum vector symbol is point-group scalar,
\begin{equation}
 G_\eta(0)=\Id_d\otimes g_\eta,
 \qquad g_\eta\succ0;
 \label{eq:pointgroup-scalar}
\end{equation}
\item the parent density mode obeys
$E_0(\bq)\asymp\eps\abs{d(\bq)}^2$ near the origin.
\end{enumerate}
Then for every normalized transverse source,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{(d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(\bq)t(\bq)}
 \le C\eta\abs{\bq}^2,
 \label{eq:q2-mixing}
\end{equation}
so its hydrodynamic current $H_{-1}$ weight satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \langle J_\eta,H_\eta^+J_\eta\rangle
 \le C\eps\eta^2\abs{\bq}^2.
 \label{eq:hminusone-q2}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the soft spectral tail obeys
\begin{equation}
 \sup_L\frac1{V_L}
 \langle J_{L,T},H_L^+\mathbf1_{(0,\delta]}(H_L)J_{L,T}\rangle
 \le C\eta^2\delta+o_\delta(1).
 \label{eq:tail-delta}
\end{equation}
It is therefore $H_{-1}$-tight.  The uniform Kohn kernel, the
thermodynamic-first Abelian zero-frequency kernel, and the static transverse
Meissner kernel exist together and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 D_K(t)=D_A(t)=D_M(t)
 =\eps\,t(0)^\dagger(\Id_d\otimes g_\eta)t(0)
 +D_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0;t).}
 \label{eq:isotropic-identification}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 D_M=\lambda^6\Delta D_{\rm parent}+O(\lambda^8\Delta).
 \label{eq:isotropic-transfer}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Quasi-locality gives
$\norm{G_\eta(\bq)-G_\eta(0)}\le C\eta\abs{\bq}$.  By
\eqref{eq:transverse-symbol} and \eqref{eq:pointgroup-scalar},
\begin{equation}
 (d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(0)t
 =(d^\dagger\otimes g_\eta)t=0.
\end{equation}
Since $\norm{d(\bq)}=O(\abs\bq)$, the remaining difference gives
\eqref{eq:q2-mixing}.  Dividing the squared current amplitude by the
quadratic energy proves \eqref{eq:hminusone-q2}.  Restricting to energies at
most $\delta$ gives $\eps\abs q^2\lesssim\delta$, hence
\eqref{eq:tail-delta}; uniform tightness of the regular sector adds only
$o_\delta(1)$ to the soft tail.

The static weighted residual in the closed sector is
\begin{equation}
 \cC_\eta(\bq;t)
 =\eps\left[
 t^\dagger G_\eta t
 -t^\dagger G_\eta(d\otimes\Id)
 \bigl((d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta(d\otimes\Id)\bigr)^+
 (d^\dagger\otimes\Id)G_\eta t
 \right].
 \label{eq:symbol-residual}
\end{equation}
The subtraction is $O(\eps\eta^2\abs q^2)$ and the first term converges to
the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:isotropic-identification}.  At $q=0$ the
gradient vanishes, so the same expression is the Kohn kernel.  The
$H_{-1}$-tightness criterion identifies it with the Abelian zero-frequency
kernel.  This proves all three limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why point-group symmetry is the natural microscopic input]
For the hypercubic point group, the spatial vector representation is
irreducible.  Any zero-momentum row metric commuting with all signed coordinate
permutations is therefore $\Id_d$ on the spatial index, though it may be an
arbitrary positive matrix on block multiplicities.  Equation
\eqref{eq:pointgroup-scalar} is thus forced by a symmetric placement of
identical control gadgets on every oriented bond.  It is not forced in a
one-dimensional construction, where there is no independent transverse
channel.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Microscopic branch floor under an isotropic safe remainder]
\label{cor:micro-floor}
For the $d\ge2$ symmetric extension of the two-block parent, suppose the
order-eight low-block remainder is a hydrodynamically closed geometric row
metric satisfying \eqref{eq:pointgroup-scalar}.  For a selected product-AGP
composition with block densities $\rho_b$, the electronic-link response obeys
\begin{equation}
 D_K=D_A=D_M
 \ge2\lambda^6\Delta
 \left(1-C\lambda^2\right)
 \sum_bj_b\rho_b(1-\rho_b).
 \label{eq:microscopic-floor}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is positive whenever at least one selected block is
nonsaturated and $\lambda$ is sufficiently small.  The pair-momentum
superfluid weight is one quarter of \eqref{eq:microscopic-floor}.
\end{corollary}

\section{A finite-cluster criterion for state-source safety}
\label{sec:row-ideal}

The abstract metric hypothesis can be tested cluster by cluster.  Let $D_X$ be
the stack of all parent rows supported in a finite connected cluster $X$, and
let
\begin{equation}
 p_X=P_{\Ker D_X},
 \qquad q_X=\Id-p_X.
 \label{eq:local-pq}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Two-sided parent-row ideal]
\label{prop:row-ideal}
For a Hermitian cluster operator $r_X$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $p_Xr_X=r_Xp_X=0$;
\item $r_X=q_Xr_Xq_X$;
\item there is a Hermitian target operator $Y_X$ such that
\begin{equation}
 r_X=D_X^\dagger Y_XD_X.
 \label{eq:local-factorization}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
One explicit choice is
\begin{equation}
 Y_X=(D_X^+)^\dagger r_XD_X^+,
 \qquad
 \norm{Y_X}\le\norm{D_X^+}^2\norm{r_X}.
 \label{eq:local-X}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence of the first two statements is immediate.  The third implies
the first because $D_Xp_X=0$.  Conversely,
$D_X^+D_X=q_X$, so \eqref{eq:local-X} gives
\begin{equation}
 D_X^\dagger Y_XD_X
 =(D_X^+D_X)^\dagger r_X(D_X^+D_X)
 =q_Xr_Xq_X=r_X.
\end{equation}
The norm bound follows by submultiplicativity.
\end{proof}

For the pair-permutation parent on a connected bounded-degree cluster, the
smallest positive eigenvalue in each fixed-pair sector equals the one-particle
graph gap by Aldous' spectral-gap theorem \cite{Caputo2010}.  A standard graph
bound then gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{D_X^+}^2\le C\abs X^2
 \label{eq:local-pseudoinverse}
\end{equation}
(up to fixed onsite and coupling constants).  Polynomial support growth is
absorbed by an exponential interaction weight: for every $0<\mu'<\mu$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_X\e^{\mu'\operatorname{diam}X}\norm{Y_X}
 \le C_{\mu-\mu'}
 \sum_X\e^{\mu\operatorname{diam}X}\norm{r_X}.
 \label{eq:locality-loss}
\end{equation}
Thus a quasi-local cluster interaction satisfying the two-sided condition
assembles into a bounded quasi-local target operator.  This is the precise
sense in which a local row-ideal audit controls the zero-field state source
uniformly in volume.

\begin{definition}[Unsafe cluster projection]
Fix the canonical connected-cluster decomposition delivered by the local
Schrieffer--Wolff recursion.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak S_X(r_X)=q_Xr_Xq_X,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak U_X(r_X)=r_X-\mathfrak S_X(r_X).
 \label{eq:safe-unsafe}
\end{equation}
The first term is state-source safe.  The second contains the local ground
compression and the cross terms that can be amplified by the closing parent
pseudoinverse.
\end{definition}

Passing the state-source audit does not by itself prove the isotropic theorem.
One must additionally examine the action of $Y_X$ on the current-created
vector representation.  A scalar convolution on row labels passes
immediately; an operator-valued row metric requires a representation audit of
the low-energy source sector.


\paragraph{Audit consequence.}
Part I reduces microscopic transfer to two finite questions.  First, each
connected Schrieffer--Wolff coefficient must have vanishing local ground
compression and cross source, equivalently it must lie in the two-sided parent
row ideal.  Second, its induced target metric on the current-created soft
sector must have point-group scalar zero-momentum symbol.  Part II now applies
this test to the \emph{complete} weighted-degree-eight coefficient.  The
result supersedes the earlier single-overlap warning
\cite{SledgianowskiSWTransfer}: the full unmodified coefficient is unsafe at
two-pair filling, while its exact counterterm is explicit.

\part{Complete weighted-degree-eight audit}

\section{Microscopic hierarchy and the degree-eight transfer question}\label{sec:hierarchy}

Let $e=(x,y)$ be an oriented nearest-neighbor edge.  Each endpoint carries two active blocks $a=1,2$ and spin $\sigma$.  The crossed bridge is
\begin{equation}
 B_e=\sum_\sigma\left(
 c_{1,y,\sigma}^\dagger c_{2,x,\sigma}
 +c_{1,x,\sigma}^\dagger c_{2,y,\sigma}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right).
 \label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
The microscopic control attached to $e$ has low state $g$ and high states $m,s,a$.  In units of the control scale $\Delta$, the weighted hierarchy is
\begin{equation}
 H(\lambda)=H_0+\lambda V_1+\lambda^2V_2
 +\lambda^4W_4+\lambda^6W_6,
 \label{eq:hierarchy}
\end{equation}
where $V_1$ contains the same- and opposite-chirality routers, $V_2$ contains $bB_e(g\leftrightarrow m)$, and the screened order-four term is
\begin{equation}
 W_4=\frac{b^2}{\bar\Delta_m}\sum_eB_e^2.
 \label{eq:W4}
\end{equation}
The degree-six term contains the base swap parent, the second matched $B^2$ coefficient, and the positive multiplicative interaction.  The product-AGP zero manifold at total pair number $n$ is
\begin{equation}
 \cZ_n=\Span\{\ket{n_1,n_2}:n_1+n_2=n\}.
 \label{eq:Z}
\end{equation}

The earlier microscopic theorem \cite{SledgianowskiMicro} gives
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{[7]}
 =\lambda^6\Delta\left(H_{\mathrm{base}}+\bar\alpha\sum_eK_e^\dagger K_e\right)
 +O_{\mathrm{loc}}(\lambda^8\Delta),
 \label{eq:degree6}
\end{equation}
with complete Peierls derivative control.  The exact parent has a closing spatial gap, so an $O_{\mathrm{loc}}(\lambda^8)$ estimate alone does not transfer the response.  The first task is to determine whether the canonical degree-eight coefficient preserves the zero manifold or creates a current-relevant state source.

\section{Complete degree-eight resolvent enumeration}\label{sec:enumeration}

Let $P$ fix every control in $g$ and $Q=1-P$.  The only low--high coupling is
\begin{equation}
 QH(\lambda)P=\lambda^2C_2,
 \qquad
 C_2=b\sum_e\ket{m_e}\bra{G}\otimes B_e.
 \label{eq:C2}
\end{equation}
Inside the high block write
\begin{equation}
 QH(\lambda)Q=H_{0,Q}+\lambda A+\lambda^2B_Q+\lambda^4C_Q+O(\lambda^6),
 \label{eq:HQ}
\end{equation}
where $A=QV_1Q$, $B_Q=QV_2Q$, and $C_Q=QW_4Q$.  Put $R_0=H_{0,Q}^{-1}$.  Since the outer bridges carry weight four in total, only the weighted-degree-four coefficient of the high resolvent can contribute to $R^{(8)}$.

\begin{proposition}[Complete single-flavor class list]
\label{prop:class-list}
The degree-four high-resolvent coefficient is
\begin{align}
 R_4={}&R_0AR_0AR_0AR_0AR_0
 +R_0B_QR_0B_QR_0-R_0C_QR_0
 \nonumber\\
 &-\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Perm}(A,A,B_Q)}
 R_0\pi_1R_0\pi_2R_0\pi_3R_0.
 \label{eq:R4}
\end{align}
The mixed $AAB_Q$ contribution vanishes between $C_2$ and $C_2^\dagger$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 R^{(8)}=-C_2^\dagger R_4C_2
 =R_{A^4}^{(8)}+R_{B_Q^2}^{(8)}+R_{C_Q}^{(8)}.
 \label{eq:R8classes}
\end{equation}
No degree-six base or second-shell insertion can contribute at weighted degree eight.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Neumann expansion of $(H_{0,Q}+X)^{-1}$ gives every ordered word whose weights sum to four, with sign $(-1)^{\text{word length}}$.  These are $1+1+1+1$, $2+2$, $4$, and $1+1+2$.  A state entering through $C_2$ has one $m$-control excitation.  Each $B_Q$ toggles the number of $m$-type control excitations by one, whereas $A$ only routes $m\leftrightarrow s,a$.  A word with one $B_Q$ cannot return a one-excitation state to the one-excitation sector required by $C_2^\dagger$, so every $AAB_Q$ word vanishes.  A weight-six insertion would give total weight at least ten after the two outer bridges.
\end{proof}

The zero-energy Feshbach coefficient is sufficient for the present audit.  On the parent zero manifold the first possible energy shift is order $\lambda^8$, while the energy derivative of the Feshbach map begins at order $\lambda^4$; energy dependence therefore changes the branch equation only at order $\lambda^{12}$.

\section{Closed degree-eight source formula}\label{sec:source}

Define
\begin{equation}
 \cB_2=\sum_eB_e^2.
 \label{eq:B2sum}
\end{equation}
The all-filling router identities imply, on the seniority-zero bridge source,
\begin{equation}
 C_{s,e}^2B_eP_0=4Q_{s,e}B_eP_0,
 \qquad
 C_{a,e}^2B_eP_0=4Q_{a,e}B_eP_0.
 \label{eq:router}
\end{equation}
Because $Q_{a,e}B_eP_{\cZ}=K_eP_{\cZ}=0$, only the same-chirality channel contributes to the unsafe zero-manifold source.

\begin{lemma}[Router source]
\label{lem:router-source}
On the complete product-AGP manifold,
\begin{equation}
 R_{A^4}^{(8)}P_{\cZ}
 =-\frac{16b^2s^4}{\bar\Delta_m^3\bar\Delta_s^2}
 \cB_2P_{\cZ}.
 \label{eq:A4source}
\end{equation}
On the complete seniority-zero space the same coefficient decomposes as
\begin{equation}
 R_{A^4}^{(8)}
 =-c_s\sum_eB_e^2+(c_s-c_a)\sum_eK_e^\dagger K_e,
 \label{eq:A4decomp}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 c_s=\frac{16b^2s^4}{\bar\Delta_m^3\bar\Delta_s^2},
 \qquad
 c_a=\frac{16b^2a^4}{\bar\Delta_m^3\bar\Delta_a^2}.
 \label{eq:csca}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A four-router path must alternate through $m$.  Mixed $s$--$a$ paths contain $Q_sQ_a$ on the routed source and vanish.  Pure $s$ and pure $a$ paths give the denominators in \eqref{eq:csca}.  Finally $B Q_sB=B^2-K^2$ and $BQ_aB=K^2$ on seniority zero.
\end{proof}

The bridge-overlap term and the high-sector feedback of the matched $W_4$ term combine more simply than either term separately.

\begin{proposition}[Linked commutator-square bridge metric]
\label{prop:M}
For the single-flavor control,
\begin{equation}
 \left(R_{B_Q^2}^{(8)}+R_{C_Q}^{(8)}\right)
 =\frac{b^4}{\bar\Delta_m^3}\cM,
 \label{eq:BCM}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \cM=\sum_eB_e^4+\frac12\sum_{e<f}
 [B_e,B_f]^\dagger[B_e,B_f].}
 \label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
Only overlapping bonds contribute to the second sum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $C_Q$ insertion gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{b^4}{\bar\Delta_m^3}\sum_{e,f}B_eB_f^2B_e.
 \label{eq:Cfeedback}
\end{equation}
The two-control bridge path gives, for each unordered pair,
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{b^4}{2\bar\Delta_m^3}\{B_e,B_f\}^2.
 \label{eq:overlap-pair}
\end{equation}
For Hermitian $E,F$,
\begin{equation}
 EF^2E+FE^2F-\frac12\{E,F\}^2
 =\frac12[E,F]^\dagger[E,F].
 \label{eq:comm-identity}
\end{equation}
The diagonal terms in \eqref{eq:Cfeedback} are $B_e^4$.  If the active supports are disjoint, the commutator vanishes, so the result is a linked local interaction rather than a disconnected product.
\end{proof}

Combining the two results gives the complete source coefficient.

\begin{theorem}[Exact degree-eight source formula]
\label{thm:source-formula}
For the unmodified single-flavor screened microscopic family,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 R^{(8)}P_{\cZ}
 =\frac{b^2}{\bar\Delta_m^3}
 \left[b^2\cM-\frac{16s^4}{\bar\Delta_s^2}\cB_2\right]P_{\cZ}.}
 \label{eq:source-formula}
\end{equation}
The closed-shell Kramers completion replaces $\cM$ by
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \cM_\Theta=\frac12\sum_eB_e^4+\frac12\sum_{e<f}
 [B_e,B_f]^\dagger[B_e,B_f].}
 \label{eq:Mtheta}
\end{equation}
The inter-bond coefficient is unchanged; double excitation of the two partners halves only the local $B_e^4$ coefficient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The single-flavor result is Lemma~\ref{lem:router-source} and Proposition~\ref{prop:M}.  In the closed shell, each of two independent Kramers channels has bridge amplitude $b/\sqrt2$.  Summing the two same-channel local paths gives $2(b/\sqrt2)^4=b^4/2$.  For two distinct bonds there are four partner pairs, each with weight $b^4/4$; their total leaves the single-flavor commutator-square coefficient unchanged.
\end{proof}

\section{Two-pair all-filling obstruction}\label{sec:obstruction}

A one-pair calculation alone is misleading.  On a three-cell ring, the one-pair compression of \eqref{eq:source-formula} is proportional to the all-ones matrix and can be canceled accidentally by
\begin{equation}
 b_*=\frac{2s^2}{\bar\Delta_s}.
 \label{eq:bstar}
\end{equation}
The cancellation does not extend to two pairs.

Let
\begin{equation}
 \ket{0,2}=\frac1{\sqrt3}\sum_{0\le x<y\le2}
 d_{2,x}^\dagger d_{2,y}^\dagger\ket0,
 \qquad
 \ket\Phi=d_{1,0}^\dagger d_{1,1}^\dagger\ket0.
 \label{eq:witness-states}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Two-pair degree-eight obstruction]
\label{thm:obstruction}
On the three-cell two-pair sector,
\begin{equation}
 \bra\Phi\cB_2\ket{0,2}=0,
 \qquad
 \bra\Phi\cM\ket{0,2}=8\sqrt3,
 \qquad
 \bra\Phi\cM_\Theta\ket{0,2}=4\sqrt3.
 \label{eq:witness}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $b\ne0$, neither the single-flavor nor the closed-shell Kramers unmodified family preserves the complete product-AGP zero manifold at weighted degree eight.  No scalar retuning of $b$, $s$, $a$, or an order-eight adjustment of the matched $B^2$ coefficient can cancel the full all-filling source while the required degree-four cancellation is retained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every term in $\cB_2$ contains two one-electron bridge hops and can transfer at most one onsite pair between blocks.  It therefore has zero matrix element from $\ket{0,2}$ to $\ket\Phi$, which has both pairs transferred.  The commutator $[B_e,B_f]$ of two number-conserving fermion bilinears is itself a bilinear.  Hence $[B_e,B_f]^\dagger[B_e,B_f]$ contains at most two one-electron transfers after normal ordering and likewise cannot connect the two witness states.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \bra\Phi\frac12\sum_{e<f}[B_e,B_f]^\dagger[B_e,B_f]
 \ket{0,2}=0.
 \label{eq:comm-witness-zero}
\end{equation}
Only the local $B_e^4$ terms contain the four transfers required to move both onsite pairs.  Direct fermionic action on the three translated bonds gives
$8\sqrt3$.  Equation \eqref{eq:comm-witness-zero} then makes the Kramers value
exactly one half, because \eqref{eq:Mtheta} halves only the local $B_e^4$
coefficient.  The router term in \eqref{eq:source-formula} is proportional to
$\cB_2$ and cannot affect this matrix element.  The separate vanishing in
\eqref{eq:comm-witness-zero} is also checked by the retained structure
certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Analytic scope of the no-go]
Below the remote control gap, the finite-volume Feshbach map is analytic in
$\lambda$.  Preservation of the complete product-AGP manifold for every
sufficiently small coupling would therefore force each weighted coefficient to
preserve it.  The nonzero matrix element in \eqref{eq:witness} rules out such an
analytic all-composition transfer.  It does not exclude a nongeneric isolated
finite value of $\lambda$ at which higher-order leakage might accidentally
cancel the degree-eight term; no such isolated cancellation is asserted or
observed here.
\end{remark}

The obstruction is stronger than a poor parameter choice.  Writing
\begin{equation}
 R^{(8)}P_{\cZ}=b^2(U+b^2V)P_{\cZ},
 \label{eq:UV}
\end{equation}
$U$ and $V$ are exactly antiparallel in the one-pair sector, but not at higher filling.  The accompanying exact finite-Fock-space certificate finds the following minimum over every scalar $b$ for the representative parameters $\bar\Delta_m=10$, $\bar\Delta_s=\bar\Delta_a=8$, $s=0.9$, $a=0.45$.

\toprule
Pairs & $\cos_F(U,V)$ & best $b$ & minimum source norm of $U+b^2V$\\
\midrule
1 & $-1$ & $0.2025$ & $<3\times10^{-12}$\\
2 & $-0.9058554866$ & $0.1181008620$ & $8.6587\times10^{-4}$\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The numerical values certify the finite matrices; \cref{thm:obstruction} is the analytic reason the higher-filling residual cannot vanish.

\section{Explicit degree-eight counterterm and truncated transfer}\label{sec:C8}

The negative result identifies a simple finite-order repair.  Define the active counterinteraction
\begin{equation}
 \cC_8=
 \frac{16b^2s^4}{\bar\Delta_m^3\bar\Delta_s^2}\sum_eB_e^2
 -\frac{b^4}{\bar\Delta_m^3}\cM
 \label{eq:C8}
\end{equation}
for the single-flavor model, and replace $\cM$ by $\cM_\Theta$ for the closed shell.

\begin{corollary}[Explicit response-safe degree-eight completion]
\label{cor:C8}
Adding $\lambda^8\Delta\cC_8$ cancels the complete degree-eight source on every product-AGP filling.  On seniority zero, the surviving degree-eight term is
\begin{equation}
 (c_s-c_a)\sum_eK_e^\dagger K_e,
 \label{eq:safe-K-shift}
\end{equation}
which is row-relative and positive whenever
$s^2/\bar\Delta_s>a^2/\bar\Delta_a$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use \eqref{eq:A4decomp}, \eqref{eq:BCM}, and \eqref{eq:C8}.  The $B^2$ and $\cM$ pieces cancel identically, leaving \eqref{eq:safe-K-shift}.
\end{proof}

This is the complete, model-specific replacement for the earlier abstract
``remove the unsafe projection and average the safe metric'' construction.
The counterterm cancels the \emph{entire} degree-eight source formula, not only
the isolated two-control overlap.  It is finite range because
$[B_e,B_f]=0$ on disjoint supports, and its surviving
$K_e^\dagger K_e$ contribution is manifestly in the two-sided parent-row
ideal of Proposition~\ref{prop:row-ideal}.

\begin{proposition}[Locality and Peierls covariance of the explicit counterterm]
\label{prop:C8-locality}
The counterinteraction \eqref{eq:C8} is finite range.  Replacing each bridge by
its fully covariant link-field version $B_e(A)$ gives a Peierls-covariant
interaction $\cC_8(A)$ whose first two field derivatives have volume-uniform
local interaction norms.  Inserted in the microscopic Hamiltonian with
coefficient $\lambda^8\Delta$, it changes the canonical low-block coefficient
at weighted degree eight by exactly $\cC_8$; conjugation corrections from a
fixed degree-seven Schrieffer--Wolff unitary begin at higher weighted degree.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The commutator term is supported only on overlapping bonds because
$[B_e,B_f]=0$ for disjoint active supports.  The remaining terms are single-bond
polynomials.  Covariant link substitution preserves these support bounds, and
two field derivatives only insert bounded displacement factors.  Finally, an
order-eight active term is acted on by the lower-order block-diagonalizing
unitary as the identity plus positive powers of $\lambda$, so its leading
low-block contribution is its own order-eight coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional response transfer through weighted degree eight]
\label{cor:degree8-transfer}
The completed coefficient satisfies the exact two-sided row-ideal criterion on
the complete product-AGP manifold.  Suppose, in addition, that its
point-group-complete $d\ge2$ Peierls lift is hydrodynamically closed on the
current-created soft sector in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:hydro-closed}.  Point-group
symmetry then makes its zero-momentum spatial vector symbol scalar, and
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 D_K^{[8]}=D_A^{[8]}=D_M^{[8]}
 =\lambda^6\Delta D_{\rm parent}+O(\lambda^8\Delta).}
 \label{eq:degree8-completed-transfer}
\end{equation}
Under $s^2/\bar\Delta_s>a^2/\bar\Delta_a$, the explicit degree-eight
multiplicative-row shift is positive.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The cancellation and surviving row-relative term are Corollary~\ref{cor:C8}.  The
additional closure hypothesis identifies that row metric with the soft
current representation used in \cref{thm:isotropic}; point-group completeness
then enforces its scalar zero-momentum spatial factor.  The isotropic theorem
proves \eqref{eq:degree8-completed-transfer}.
\end{proof}

The completion is not two-electron-only: after normal ordering, $B_e^4$
contains higher-body active interactions.  Synthesizing \eqref{eq:C8} from the
restricted exchange/correlated-hopping tensor of the original microscopic
model, or recursively completing every higher weighted order with a convergent
local map, remains open.


\part{Exact dark branch and finite-coupling Meissner floor}

\section{The exact coherent dark branch}\label{sec:dark}

The failure of the complete product manifold does not imply that every microscopic paired branch is lost.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \eta_-^+=\eta_1^+-\eta_2^+,
 \qquad
 \ket{\Omega_n^-}=\mathcal N_{L,n}(\eta_-^+)^n\ket0.
 \label{eq:dark-state}
\end{equation}
In the normalized product-composition basis,
\begin{equation}
 \ket{\Omega_n^-}
 =\frac1{\sqrt{\binom{2L}{n}}}
 \sum_{n_1+n_2=n}(-1)^{n_2}
 \sqrt{\binom{L}{n_1}\binom{L}{n_2}}
 \ket{n_1,n_2}.
 \label{eq:dark-coeff}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact bridge darkness]
\label{prop:dark}
For every edge $e$ and every filling,
\begin{equation}
 [B_e,\eta_-^+]=0,
 \qquad
 B_e\ket{\Omega_n^-}=0.
 \label{eq:darkness}
\end{equation}
Within $\cZ_n$, the common kernel of all $B_e$ is one dimensional and is spanned by $\ket{\Omega_n^-}$ on a connected lattice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppress spin and write the one-body bridge matrix as
$h_e=\tau_x\otimes A_e$, where $A_e=A_e^T$ is the endpoint adjacency and $\tau_x$ acts on the two blocks.  The singlet pairing matrix of $\eta_-^+$ is $F=\tau_z\otimes\Id\otimes\I\sigma_y$.  For a one-body operator $c^\dagger h c$,
\begin{equation}
 [c^\dagger hc,\eta_F^+]
 =\frac12c^\dagger(hF+Fh^T)c^{\dagger T}.
\end{equation}
Since $\{\tau_x,\tau_z\}=0$, the commutator vanishes.  Also $B_e\ket0=0$, proving the second identity.

For uniqueness, put $r=n_1$ and $n_2=n-r$.  Direct hard-core reduction and AGP combinatorics give, on the periodic $L$-edge lattice,
\begin{align}
 \bra{r,n-r}\sum_eB_e^2\ket{r,n-r}
 &=4n-\frac{8r(n-r)}{L},
 \label{eq:B2-jacobi-diag}\\
 \bra{r+1,n-r-1}\sum_eB_e^2\ket{r,n-r}
 &=\frac4L\sqrt{(r+1)(L-r)(n-r)(L-n+r+1)}.
 \label{eq:B2-jacobi-off}
\end{align}
The product-AGP composition states are invariant under independent permutations
of the $L$ sites within each block.  Consequently, the compression of $B_e^2$
is identical for every edge $e$.  For an arbitrary graph with $|E|>0$, the
compression of $\sum_{e\in E}B_e^2$ is therefore $|E|/L$ times
\eqref{eq:B2-jacobi-diag}--\eqref{eq:B2-jacobi-off}.  Its off-diagonal entries
are strictly positive at every interior point of the allowed composition
interval, so the compression is an irreducible Hermitian tridiagonal matrix.
Such a Jacobi matrix has simple eigenvalues.  Equation \eqref{eq:darkness}
supplies a zero vector, hence the common bridge kernel in $\cZ_n$ is one
dimensional.  In particular, no translation-invariance assumption is needed
for the connected-lattice statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All-orders zero-field eigenbranch]
\label{cor:allorders-dark}
In the screened microscopic family, with all controls in their $g$ states,
\begin{equation}
 \ket{\Omega_n^-}\otimes\ket G
 \label{eq:micro-dark}
\end{equation}
is an exact zero-energy eigenstate at $A=0$ for every finite coupling $\lambda$ for which the Hamiltonian is defined.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The base parent annihilates every vector in $\cZ_n$.  Every bridge vertex contains $B_e$ and annihilates \eqref{eq:micro-dark}; every direct shell term contains $B_e^2$ and also annihilates it.  Router vertices act only after a control has left $g$, which never occurs on the dark state.  The screened monopole terms cancel by construction.
\end{proof}

The corollary does not by itself prove that the exact eigenbranch is the ground state of the original matched model.  The finite-order matching does not make the complete microscopic Hamiltonian positive semidefinite.  The next construction supplies that missing property without using a closing-gap perturbation theorem.

\section{Positive-semidefinite finite-coupling completion}\label{sec:psd}

Consider one control edge.  After separating the low control state $g$ from the complete high control space, write its local Hamiltonian as
\begin{equation}
 h_e(0)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \beta B_e^2&t_BB_eP_m^\dagger\\
 t_BP_mB_e&D_e
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D_e\succeq d_*\Id>0,
 \label{eq:local-block}
\end{equation}
where $P_m$ inserts the bridge source into the $m$ component of the high block.  The router is contained in $D_e$.

\begin{theorem}[Strict overcompensation]
\label{thm:overcomp}
If
\begin{equation}
 \beta>\frac{t_B^2}{d_*},
 \label{eq:beta-strict}
\end{equation}
then $h_e(0)\succeq0$.  Moreover, every zero vector of $h_e(0)$ has its control in $g$ and satisfies $B_e\psi=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Schur complement of $D_e$ is
\begin{equation}
 S_e=\beta B_e^2-t_B^2B_eP_m^\dagger D_e^{-1}P_mB_e.
 \end{equation}
Since $D_e^{-1}\preceq d_*^{-1}\Id$,
\begin{equation}
 S_e\succeq\left(\beta-\frac{t_B^2}{d_*}\right)B_e^2\succeq0.
 \label{eq:Schur-bound}
\end{equation}
The standard block Schur criterion gives $h_e\succeq0$.  Equality in the strict bound forces $B_e\psi=0$, and the high component $-D_e^{-1}t_BP_mB_e\psi$ then vanishes.
\end{proof}

Define the exact finite-coupling family
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{PSD}}(0)
 =\lambda^6\Delta H_{\mathrm{base}}+\sum_e h_e(0),
 \label{eq:HPSD}
\end{equation}
with the screened density channel and the strict coefficient \eqref{eq:beta-strict}.

\begin{corollary}[Exact fixed-number ground state]
\label{cor:PSD-ground}
On a connected lattice, $H_{\mathrm{PSD}}(0)$ is frustration free and has the unique fixed-$2n$ zero state \eqref{eq:micro-dark} at every interior filling.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every term is positive and annihilates the dark state.  Conversely, a global zero vector lies in the base-parent zero manifold and, by \cref{thm:overcomp}, obeys $B_e\psi=0$ on every edge with every control in $g$.  Proposition \ref{prop:dark} gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}

Under the complete Peierls lift, each single-edge block $h_e(A_e)$ is
unitarily equivalent to its zero-field version by a gauge conjugation of the
active endpoint modes and the control source.  The conjugating unitary need not
be supported on the edge alone, and no claim is made that a nonzero loop flux is
globally pure gauge; only the termwise unitary equivalence of $h_e(A_e)$ is used.
Hence $h_e(A_e)\succeq0$ for every edge phase, and the full family remains a sum
of local positive terms.  This is a nonperturbative microscopic parent rather
than a Schrieffer--Wolff approximation.

\section{Finite-coupling microscopic Meissner floor}\label{sec:micro-floor}

Let the spatial lattice have $d\ge2$, include every point-group-related edge orientation, and couple a physical transverse edge field.  Factor the positive Hamiltonian into a direct-sum row map
\begin{equation}
 \cD_{\mathrm{full}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \lambda^3\sqrt\Delta\,\cD_{\mathrm{sw}}\\
 \cD_{\mathrm{gad}}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \cS_{\mathrm{full}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \lambda^3\sqrt\Delta\,\cS_{\mathrm{sw}}\\
 \cS_{\mathrm{gad}}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:row-stack}
\end{equation}
For every divergence-free field, the graph-Hodge identity of
\cite{SledgianowskiMeissner} gives
\begin{equation}
 \cD_{\mathrm{sw}}^\dagger\cS_{\mathrm{sw}}=0.
 \label{eq:sw-kernel}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-coupling transverse response floor]
\label{thm:floor}
For the exact PSD microscopic family and the selected dark branch, every finite-volume static curvature and imaginary-frequency transverse kernel satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \cK_{L,T}(q,\I\zeta)
 \ge\cC_{L,T}(q)
 \ge\lambda^6\Delta\,\norm{\cS_{\mathrm{sw},T}(q)}^2,
 \qquad \zeta\ge0.
 \label{eq:floor-operator}
\end{equation}
If both block swap couplings are $j_1,j_2$, then per spatial volume
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 D_{\mathrm{sw},L}^{(-)}
 =(j_1+j_2)\frac{n(2L-n)}{L(2L-1)}.}
 \label{eq:finite-floor}
\end{equation}
Consequently, with $n/(2L)\to\rho\in(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
  \liminf D_K\ge D_*,\qquad
  \liminf D_A\ge D_*,\qquad
  \liminf D_M\ge D_*,\\
  D_*=2\lambda^6\Delta(j_1+j_2)\rho(1-\rho)>0
 \end{gathered}}
 \label{eq:thermo-floor}
\end{equation}
The pair-phase superfluid-weight floor is one quarter of \eqref{eq:thermo-floor}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The static positive-square curvature is the least-squares residual
\begin{equation}
 \cC=\min_X\norm{\cS_{\mathrm{full}}-\cD_{\mathrm{full}}X}^2.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:sw-kernel},
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\cS_{\mathrm{sw}}-\cD_{\mathrm{sw}}X}^2
 =\norm{\cS_{\mathrm{sw}}}^2+\norm{\cD_{\mathrm{sw}}X}^2,
\end{equation}
so no state correction, including one using the gadget sector, can cancel the transverse swap source.  The finite-frequency kernel exceeds the static residual by the positive spectral defect, proving \eqref{eq:floor-operator}.

The composition probabilities in \eqref{eq:dark-coeff} form a hypergeometric distribution.  Averaging the exact blockwise swap response
$2j_an_a(L-n_a)/[L(L-1)]$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[n_a(L-n_a)]
 =\frac{n(L-1)(2L-n)}{2(2L-1)},
\end{equation}
which yields \eqref{eq:finite-floor}.  Taking any admissible thermodynamic, zero-frequency, and transverse zero-momentum limits gives \eqref{eq:thermo-floor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the floor does and does not identify]
The theorem is already a genuine microscopic zero-frequency Meissner statement: the static transverse response is uniformly positive at every allowed $q$, and the thermodynamic-first imaginary-frequency kernel has the same positive floor.  It does not assert that the additional gadget contribution has identical Kohn, Abelian, and transverse limits.  Proving equality for the \emph{total} response still requires the $H_{-1}$ tightness and point-group closure conditions developed in Part I.
\end{remark}


\section{Conclusion and remaining microscopic problem}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The merged analysis separates three statements that had previously been
conflated.

First, the infrared transfer problem has a clean functional-analytic answer.
A local remainder must control both the zero-field state source and the current
source.  The two-sided row ideal eliminates the former.  Target-metric
factorization controls the latter uniformly through a closing parent gap, but
only point-group isotropy converts bounded soft-mode weight into the
$H_{-1}$ tightness required for exact Kohn--Abelian--Meissner identification.
The anisotropic counterexample shows that this distinction is sharp.

Second, the unmodified matched microscopic family fails the stronger
all-composition objective.  The complete degree-eight source contains a linked
fourth-power bridge metric that cannot be canceled by any scalar $B^2$ retuning
at two-pair filling.  The no-go survives the closed-shell Kramers completion.
The explicit counterterm \eqref{eq:C8} repairs the entire coefficient through
weighted degree eight and leaves a positive parent-row correction, thereby
providing a precise finite-order state-source completion rather than an
abstract existence claim.  Equality of the three truncated response limits then
follows under the separate hydrodynamic-closure and point-group symbol audit of
Part I.

Third, a different finite-coupling objective is already achieved.  The coherent
dark AGP is annihilated by every primitive bridge.  Strict local
Schur-complement overcompensation makes it the exact ground branch of a
positive-semidefinite microscopic family, and the swap backbone supplies the
nonzero transverse floor \eqref{eq:thermo-floor}.  This is a genuine
zero-temperature, zero-frequency microscopic Meissner statement for a selected
branch, although it is a lower-bound theorem rather than a complete equality
formula for the gadget response.

The remaining all-composition problem is now an analytic synthesis problem,
not a thermodynamic numerical scan: realize $\cC_8$ using restricted
microscopic two-electron ancillary channels and determine whether the resulting
sequence of row-ideal counterterms admits a convergent local Feshbach or
Schrieffer--Wolff normal form.  Independently, applying the Part I isotropy
theorem to every extra row of the positive dark-branch family would upgrade its
strict floor to a full equality theorem for the total response.

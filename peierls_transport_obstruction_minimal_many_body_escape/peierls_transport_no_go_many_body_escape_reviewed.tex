\title{\textbf{Peierls-Transport Obstruction and the Minimal Many-Body Escape}\\[0.35em]
\large Exact multi-block one-body no-go theorem and a fully covariant local null-row model}
\author{Nicholas Sledgianowski}
\date{Review-integrated revision, July 26, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove a sharp obstruction to composition-space hopping in exact multi-block
projected-Hubbard and quantum-geometric-nesting parents.  If the complete zero
manifold contains the separate one-pair antisymmetrized-geminal-power states,
then every number-conserving one-body null row is block preserving already at
zero twist.  A complete covering-lattice Peierls connection transports the
isolated interaction singular subbundles by one common analytic unitary.  The
resulting commutator source lies in the range of the unperturbed null map and
is removed exactly by the frustration-free least-squares projection.  The
surviving source preserves every block charge, so the exact composition-space
curvature operator has no off-diagonal entries.  Fixed finite range or uniform
exponential locality removes the only finite-torus alias loophole.  The blocks
may be interaction subbundles rather than normal-state energy bands.

We then give a strictly nearest-neighbor, translation-invariant, positive-square
many-body null-row model with two extensive inequivalent blocks, a complete
product-AGP zero manifold in every even sector, a termwise covariant Peierls
lift, and an exact non-diagonal one-pair composition operator
\[
 Q^{(1)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2j_1+4g^2&-4g^2\\
 -4g^2&2j_2+4g^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The escape is minimal at the level of theorem hypotheses: every assumption is
retained except that the transfer-generating null row is multiplicative and
many body rather than additive and one body.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Quantum-geometric nesting (QGN) and projected positive-semidefinite Hubbard
constructions provide frustration-free superconducting parents whose paired
zero states and flat-connection curvatures can be computed exactly
\cite{Parent2026}.  With one interaction singular block, the fixed-number
zero state is a single antisymmetrized geminal power (AGP).  With two or more
inequivalent extensive singular subbundles, the zero manifold instead contains
one product AGP for every allowed distribution of pairs among the blocks
\cite{Addendum2026}.

This degeneracy makes the action of electromagnetic flux qualitatively
important.  If the physical twist preserves each block charge, the
fixed-total-number response is only the lower envelope of independent
composition branches.  A genuine transfer source would instead produce an
operator on the composition lattice: neighboring compositions would hybridize,
and the physical curvature would be the smallest eigenvalue of that matrix
rather than the minimum of its diagonal entries.  Finding a local microscopic
realization of this composition hopping was therefore the final unresolved
step in the two-block program.

Two natural one-body candidates failed for covariance reasons.  A smooth common
hybridization of the blocks produces only a removable frame-transport
commutator, while a band-filtered multi-site bridge remains a zero row when the
bridge kernel and the projectors are Peierls twisted consistently
\cite{TwoBlock2026}.  The purpose of this note is to decide whether those
failures were accidental.  We prove that they are structural throughout the
exact local one-body class, including interaction-defined blocks that need not
coincide with normal-state energy bands.  We then construct a strictly
range-one many-body null row that keeps every other desired hypothesis and
produces an exact non-diagonal composition response.

\section{Multi-block zero manifold and curvature map}

Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak h=\bigoplus_{a=1}^q\mathfrak h_a,
 \qquad \dim\mathfrak h_a=2L_a,
 \label{eq:h-decomp}
\end{equation}
and let each block carry a skew-unitary pairing form $J_a$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \eta_a^+=\frac12c^\dagger J_ac^\dagger,
 \qquad
 |\bn\rangle
 =\bigotimes_{a=1}^q
 \left[\frac{(L_a-n_a)!}{n_a!L_a!}\right]^{1/2}
 (\eta_a^+)^{n_a}|0\rangle.
 \label{eq:product-agp}
\end{equation}
At fixed total pair number $n$, put
\begin{equation}
 \cC_n=\{\bn:0\le n_a\le L_a,\ \sum_an_a=n\},
 \qquad
 \cZ_n=\Span\{|\bn\rangle:\bn\in\cC_n\}.
 \label{eq:zero-manifold}
\end{equation}

Consider an analytic positive-square family
\begin{equation}
 H(A)=\frac12D(A)^\dagger D(A)
 =\frac12\sum_\lambda S_\lambda(A)^\dagger S_\lambda(A).
 \label{eq:H-positive}
\end{equation}
Assume that $\cZ_n=\ker D(0)$ in the sector under consideration and that the
zero cluster is separated from the positive spectrum at the fixed finite size.
For a real twist direction $v$, define
\begin{equation}
 \cB_v|\psi\rangle
 =\bigl(\partial_vS_\lambda(0)|\psi\rangle\bigr)_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \Pi=P_{\ker D(0)^\dagger}.
 \label{eq:source-map}
\end{equation}
Degenerate frustration-free perturbation theory gives the exact second-order
operator
\begin{equation}
 Q_v^{(n)}=T_v^\dagger T_v,
 \qquad
 T_v=\Pi\cB_v|_{\cZ_n}.
 \label{eq:Q-T}
\end{equation}
Thus composition hopping is present precisely when projected source vectors
from two different product-AGP compositions have nonzero overlap.

For comparison with the multi-block reduction, write
\begin{equation}
 b_{\lambda v}^{ab}=P_a\,\partial_vs_\lambda(0)\,P_b,
 \qquad
 \bm{\zeta}_v^{a\leftarrow b}
 =\bigl(d\Gamma(b_{\lambda v}^{ab})|\be_b\rangle\bigr)_\lambda
 \quad(a\ne b).
 \label{eq:transfer-seed-def}
\end{equation}
Let $\Pi_{ab}$ denote the restriction of the target-kernel projector to the
sector with one fermion in each of blocks $a$ and $b$.  The reduced transfer
norm and opposite-direction overlap are
\begin{equation}
 k_v^{a\leftarrow b}
 =\|\Pi_{ab}\bm{\zeta}_v^{a\leftarrow b}\|^2,
 \qquad
 \omega_v^{ab}
 =\left\langle
 \Pi_{ab}\bm{\zeta}_v^{b\leftarrow a},
 \Pi_{ab}\bm{\zeta}_v^{a\leftarrow b}
 \right\rangle.
 \label{eq:transfer-data-def}
\end{equation}
For two blocks at fixed total filling, $\omega_v^{ab}$ multiplies the only
possible off-diagonal matrix element between neighboring compositions
$\bn$ and $\bn+\be_a-\be_b$.  The resulting composition matrix is tridiagonal,
so we call these nearest-neighbor entries \emph{Jacobi hopping}.

\section{One-pair rigidity of one-body parents}

\begin{lemma}[Separate one-pair zero modes force block preservation]
\label{lem:one-pair-rigidity}
Let a zero-twist null row be a number-conserving one-body operator
$S=d\Gamma(s)$.  Suppose
\begin{equation}
 S|\be_b\rangle=0
 \label{eq:annihilate-one-pair}
\end{equation}
for the normalized one-pair AGP in every paired block $b$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 P_asP_b=0
 \qquad(a\ne b)
 \label{eq:one-body-block-diag}
\end{equation}
between all paired blocks.  If $S$ is Hermitian, the same conclusion includes
any unpaired complement coupled to a paired block.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix ordered blocks $a\ne b$, put
\begin{equation}
 x=P_asP_b,
 \qquad
 B_x=c_a^\dagger xc_b,
\end{equation}
and project $S|\be_b\rangle$ onto the charge sector with one fermion in block
$a$ and one in block $b$.  That component is exactly $B_x|\be_b\rangle$.
The target block is empty in $|\be_b\rangle$.  Moreover,
$|\be_b\rangle=L_b^{-1/2}\eta_b^+|0\rangle$, and a direct contraction gives
\begin{equation}
 \langle\be_b|c_{b,i}^\dagger c_{b,j}|\be_b\rangle
 =\frac{(J_bJ_b^\dagger)_{ji}}{L_b}
 =\frac{\delta_{ij}}{L_b},
 \label{eq:onepair-rdm}
\end{equation}
where the last equality is exactly the skew-unitarity of $J_b$.  Therefore
\begin{align}
 \|B_x|\be_b\rangle\|^2
 &=\langle\be_b|
 c_b^\dagger x^\dagger c_ac_a^\dagger xc_b
 |\be_b\rangle\nonumber\\
 &=\frac1{L_b}\Tr(x^\dagger x).
 \label{eq:transfer-norm}
\end{align}
The left side vanishes by \eqref{eq:annihilate-one-pair}, so $x=0$.
Repeating the argument for every ordered pair proves
\eqref{eq:one-body-block-diag}.  For a complement without its own pair state,
Hermiticity supplies the reverse matrix block.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Stronger than Hermitian projected Hubbard]
The paired-block part of the lemma does not require Hermiticity.  If the
complete zero manifold contains the separate one-pair AGPs, no additive
one-body row in a general $S^\dagger S$ factorization can hide interblock
transfer at zero twist.  A many-body row can annihilate the same states through
a multiplicative null mechanism; a one-body row cannot.
\end{remark}

\section{Peierls transport of exact interaction subbundles}

Let the finite tori be restrictions of fixed translation-covariant
covering-lattice kernels.  Let $P_a(k)$ be isolated analytic projectors onto
interaction singular subbundles.  The factor label $\lambda$ may include a
cell, microscopic channel, and positive-kernel factorization index.  Assume
that the zero-twist one-body kernels obey
\begin{equation}
 P_a(k)s_\lambda(k,k')P_b(k')=0,
 \qquad a\ne b,
 \label{eq:kernel-block-identity}
\end{equation}
for all $k,k'$ on the Brillouin torus.

In a covering-lattice Bloch convention, a complete uniform Peierls connection
shifts every momentum argument of every one-body kernel,
\begin{equation}
 s_\lambda(A;k,k')=s_\lambda(k+A,k'+A),
 \label{eq:complete-peierls-shift}
\end{equation}
with fixed orbital-embedding factors included in the symbols.  Equation
\eqref{eq:kernel-block-identity}, evaluated at $(k+A,k'+A)$, says that the
twisted row is block diagonal in the shifted decomposition $P_a(k+A)$.

Because the projectors are isolated and analytic, they admit one transport
unitary common to every factor label.  To make this explicit, adjoin the
orthogonal complement $P_0=I-\sum_aP_a$ and, along a directional path
$A=tv$, write $P_\alpha(t;k)=P_\alpha(k+tv)$.  The anti-Hermitian Kato
generator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K_v(t;k)
 =\frac12\sum_\alpha
 [\dot P_\alpha(t;k),P_\alpha(t;k)]
 =\sum_\alpha\dot P_\alpha(t;k)P_\alpha(t;k)
 \label{eq:kato-generator}
\end{equation}
satisfies $[\mathcal K_v,P_\alpha]=\dot P_\alpha$.  Hence the fiberwise
solution
\begin{equation}
 \dot R_v(t;k)=\mathcal K_v(t;k)R_v(t;k),
 \qquad R_v(0;k)=I,
 \label{eq:kato-ode}
\end{equation}
obeys
\begin{equation}
 R_v(t;k)P_\alpha(k)R_v(t;k)^\dagger=P_\alpha(k+tv)
 \label{eq:kato-transport}
\end{equation}
for the entire mutually orthogonal projector family
\cite[Chap.~II]{Kato1976}.  The dependence on $k$ is suppressed below.  The
crucial point is that $R_v$ depends only on the common block decomposition,
not on $\lambda$.  Let $\widehat R_v(t)$ be its number-conserving Fock lift.

\section{Sharp one-body Peierls-transport no-go theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Peierls-transport obstruction]
\label{thm:one-body-no-go}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(H\arabic*)},leftmargin=3.8em]
 \item $H(A)$ has the analytic positive-square form \eqref{eq:H-positive};
 \item every $S_\lambda(A)$ is a number-conserving one-body row;
 \item the same zero-twist factor family annihilates every separate one-pair
       state $|\be_a\rangle$, and at total pair number $n$ its complete kernel
       is the product-AGP span \eqref{eq:zero-manifold};
 \item the interaction singular subbundles are isolated and analytic; and
 \item the physical field dependence is the complete covering-lattice Peierls
       shift of fixed finite-range or uniformly exponentially local kernels.
\end{enumerate}
Then the exact curvature map preserves every block composition.  Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 (Q_v^{(n)})_{\mathbf m\mathbf n}=0
 \quad\text{for every }\mathbf m\ne\mathbf n.}
 \label{eq:no-offdiag-Q}
\end{equation}
In the reduced multi-block notation,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 k_v^{a\leftarrow b}=0,
 \qquad
 \omega_v^{ab}=0
 \quad(a\ne b).}
 \label{eq:no-transfer-data}
\end{equation}
Thus a physical Peierls twist may generate distinct nonzero block masses,
diagonal block curvatures, and a nontrivial lower envelope over compositions,
but it cannot generate Jacobi hopping between exact one-body interaction
blocks.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:one-pair-rigidity}, every zero-twist row preserves the exact block
decomposition.  Under the complete Peierls shift it preserves the shifted
decomposition.  Pull the shifted family back with the common Kato unitary:
\begin{equation}
 S_\lambda(tv)
 =\widehat R_v(t)\widetilde S_\lambda(tv)\widehat R_v(t)^\dagger,
 \label{eq:transport-factorization}
\end{equation}
where each $\widetilde S_\lambda(tv)$ preserves the fixed charges $N_a$.

Write
\begin{equation}
 G_v=\partial_t\widehat R_v(t)|_{t=0},
 \qquad
 \widetilde B_{\lambda v}^{\rm bp}
 =\partial_t\widetilde S_\lambda(tv)|_{t=0}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:transport-factorization} gives
\begin{equation}
 B_{\lambda v}
 =[G_v,S_\lambda]+\widetilde B_{\lambda v}^{\rm bp}.
 \label{eq:source-split}
\end{equation}
For any $|\psi\rangle\in\cZ_n$,
\begin{equation}
 [G_v,S_\lambda]|\psi\rangle
 =-S_\lambda G_v|\psi\rangle.
\end{equation}
After stacking the factor labels,
\begin{equation}
 \bigl([G_v,S_\lambda]|\psi\rangle\bigr)_\lambda
 =-D(0)G_v|\psi\rangle
 \in\operatorname{ran}D(0).
 \label{eq:comm-source-range}
\end{equation}
The least-squares projector $\Pi=P_{\ker D(0)^\dagger}$ annihilates this
entire common-transport source.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 T_v
 =\Pi\bigl(\widetilde B_{\lambda v}^{\rm bp}\bigr)_\lambda
 \big|_{\cZ_n}.
 \label{eq:T-bp}
\end{equation}
Every surviving row commutes with every fixed block charge.  The zero-twist
map $D(0)$ therefore intertwines the block-charge action on Fock space with the
diagonal action on its row target.  Consequently $\operatorname{ran}D(0)$,
$\ker D(0)^\dagger$, and the projector $\Pi$ are all invariant under those
charges.  It follows that $T_v|\bn\rangle$ and
$T_v|\mathbf m\rangle$ lie in orthogonal target charge sectors whenever
$\bn\ne\mathbf m$.  Their inner product is zero, proving
\eqref{eq:no-offdiag-Q}.  Equation \eqref{eq:no-transfer-data} is the same
statement expressed through the projected transfer seeds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Factor-gauge invariance]
An analytic unitary rotation among factor labels contributes a term proportional to
$D(0)|\psi\rangle$, which vanishes on the zero manifold.  The theorem is therefore
independent of the chosen square root of a positive interaction kernel.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The blocks need not be energy bands]
No normal-state spectral projector occurs in the proof.  The $P_a(k)$ may be
eigenprojectors of an interaction Gram operator or any other isolated
interaction-defined singular decomposition.  Forcing them to coincide with
energy bands is unnecessary.
\end{remark}

\section{Locality and the finite-torus loophole}

On one isolated finite torus, an off-block kernel can vanish only on the
sampled momentum grid because of an alias or circumference-winding
cancellation.  A continuous shift of those samples can reveal a derivative.

That is not a fixed-local thermodynamic realization.  For a common finite-range
or uniformly exponentially local infinite-lattice kernel, the matrix-valued
functions
\begin{equation}
 F_\lambda^{ab}(k,k')
 =P_a(k)s_\lambda(k,k')P_b(k')
\end{equation}
are continuous, and in the exponential case analytic in a strip.  If they
vanish on the momentum grids of an unbounded regular sequence of tori, they
vanish on a dense subset of the Brillouin torus and hence vanish identically.
The full kernel identity \eqref{eq:kernel-block-identity} follows.

Therefore the only finite-torus evasion is nonuniform: its displacement
support or Fourier harmonic must scale with the circumference.  This is a
winding/lift obstruction rather than a local one-body counterexample.

\section{Minimal algebraic escape}

\cref{lem:one-pair-rigidity} identifies what must change.  An additive
one-body row cannot contain interblock transfer while annihilating both
separate one-pair AGPs.  The smallest structural replacement is a
multiplicative null row
\begin{equation}
 K=\Pi_{\rm null}B,
 \label{eq:multiplicative-row}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is an interblock transfer bilinear, $\Pi_{\rm null}$ is a local
many-body projector annihilating the zero manifold, and
$[\Pi_{\rm null},B]=0$.  The transfer is present in the operator but hidden at
zero twist behind a null projector.  This mechanism is impossible for an
additive one-body row and is exactly where \cref{thm:one-body-no-go} stops.

The construction below is minimal at the level of theorem hypotheses: it
retains translation invariance, strict locality, number conservation,
positivity, an auditable complete zero manifold, isolated extensive blocks,
and a complete Peierls twist.  Only the condition ``every null row is one
body'' is dropped.  We do not claim minimality in orbital count or polynomial
degree.

\section{Strictly local covariant many-body null-row model}

Take a periodic chain
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_V=\mathbb Z/V\mathbb Z,
 \qquad V\ge2,
\end{equation}
with two spinful blocks $a=1,2$.  Let
\begin{equation}
 p_{a,x}^\dagger
 =c_{a,x,\uparrow}^\dagger c_{a,x,\downarrow}^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \eta_a^+=\sum_xp_{a,x}^\dagger.
 \label{eq:pair-ops}
\end{equation}
Each block has pair capacity $L_a=V$.  The blocks are made inequivalent by
choosing distinct positive interaction couplings
\begin{equation}
 j_1\ne j_2>0.
 \label{eq:distinct-couplings}
\end{equation}
Thus no block-exchange symmetry identifies their one-pair curvatures; no
additional weighted pairing form is needed in the Hamiltonian.

\subsection{Local rows}

The onsite seniority projector is
\begin{equation}
 q_{a,x}=(n_{a,x,\uparrow}-n_{a,x,\downarrow})^2.
 \label{eq:q-row}
\end{equation}
Let $W_{a,x}$ be the fermionic unitary that swaps the two complete spinful
sites $(a,x)$ and $(a,x+1)$, and define
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{a,x}=\frac{1-W_{a,x}}2.
 \label{eq:swap-projector}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
 W_{12,x}=W_{1,x}W_{2,x},
 \qquad
 \Pi_{12,x}=\frac{1-W_{12,x}}2.
 \label{eq:sim-swap}
\end{equation}
Define the crossed interblock transfer
\begin{equation}
 X_x=\sum_\sigma
 \left(
 c_{1,x+1,\sigma}^\dagger c_{2,x,\sigma}
 +c_{1,x,\sigma}^\dagger c_{2,x+1,\sigma}
 \right),
 \qquad
 B_x=X_x+X_x^\dagger.
 \label{eq:B-cross}
\end{equation}
The simultaneous swap exchanges the two terms in $X_x$, so
$[\Pi_{12,x},B_x]=0$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 K_x=\Pi_{12,x}B_x=B_x\Pi_{12,x}
 \label{eq:K-row}
\end{equation}
is Hermitian, number conserving, and supported on one nearest-neighbor bond.

\subsection{Complete bondwise Peierls lift}

Orient every covering-lattice bond as $x\to x+1$.  Put
\begin{equation}
 Y_{a,x}=n_{a,x+1,\uparrow}+n_{a,x+1,\downarrow},
 \qquad
 Y_x=Y_{1,x}+Y_{2,x}.
\end{equation}
For the boundary bond, $x+1$ means the lifted right endpoint even though its
torus label is zero.  The accumulated phase around the ring is therefore
$\Phi=VA$: this is the standard flux-threading twist, not a globally removable
pure gauge.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{a,x}(A)
 =e^{-\I AY_{a,x}}\Pi_{a,x}e^{\I AY_{a,x}},
 \qquad
 K_x(A)=e^{-\I AY_x}K_xe^{\I AY_x}.
 \label{eq:bond-peierls}
\end{equation}
Every monomial in the complete swap projector and in the complete product row
receives its covering-lattice Peierls phase.  Nothing is held fixed while
another part is twisted.

The Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 H_V(A)=
 \frac12\sum_{a,x}(\sqrt U\,q_{a,x})^2
 +\frac12\sum_{a,x}(\sqrt{j_a}\,\Pi_{a,x}(A))^2
 +\frac12\sum_x(gK_x(A))^2,}
 \label{eq:escape-H}
\end{equation}
with $U,j_1,j_2>0$ and $g\ne0$.  It is translation invariant and strictly
range one, uniformly in $V$.

\section{Complete zero manifold of the escape model}

\begin{theorem}[Exact kernel]
\label{thm:escape-kernel}
At total particle number $2n$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \ker H_V(0)|_{2n}
 =\Span\left\{
 |n_1,n_2\rangle:
 n_1+n_2=n,\ 0\le n_a\le V
 \right\}.}
 \label{eq:escape-kernel}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \dim\ker H_V(0)|_{2n}
 =\min(V,n)-\max(0,n-V)+1.}
 \label{eq:escape-nullity}
\end{equation}
Every odd-particle sector has zero nullity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because the Hamiltonian is a sum of positive squares, a zero state is
annihilated by every row.  The equations $q_{a,x}|\psi\rangle=0$ force every
local block orbital to be empty or doubly occupied.  Thus each block reduces
to a hard-core pair configuration.

On this seniority-zero subspace, $W_{a,x}$ acts as the ordinary adjacent
transposition of the pair occupations at $x$ and $x+1$.  The equations
$\Pi_{a,x}|\psi\rangle=0$ make the state invariant under every adjacent
transposition.  These transpositions generate the full permutation group
$S_V$.  At fixed block filling $n_a$, its invariant subspace is one
dimensional and is spanned by
\begin{equation}
 |n_a\rangle_a
 =\binom V{n_a}^{-1/2}
 \sum_{|A|=n_a}\prod_{x\in A}p_{a,x}^\dagger|0\rangle
 \propto(\eta_a^+)^{n_a}|0\rangle.
 \label{eq:dicke-agp}
\end{equation}
Hence the first two row families already have exactly the product-AGP span in
\eqref{eq:escape-kernel}.

Every product AGP is separately invariant under $W_{1,x}$ and $W_{2,x}$,
hence under $W_{12,x}$.  Therefore
$\Pi_{12,x}|n_1,n_2\rangle=0$.  Using
$K_x=B_x\Pi_{12,x}$, every cross row also annihilates every product AGP.
Adding the cross squares cannot create new zero modes and removes none of the
existing ones.  This proves \eqref{eq:escape-kernel}.  Counting the allowed
compositions gives \eqref{eq:escape-nullity}.  Odd particle number is
incompatible with vanishing onsite seniority on every local orbital.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact non-diagonal composition curvature}

Work in the one-pair zero basis
\begin{equation}
 |1\rangle=|1,0\rangle,
 \qquad
 |2\rangle=|0,1\rangle.
 \label{eq:onepair-basis}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
 |a;x\rangle=p_{a,x}^\dagger|0\rangle,
 \qquad
 |a\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt V}\sum_x|a;x\rangle.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Swap-row sources}

For the oriented bond $L=x$, $R=x+1$, define
\begin{equation}
 |\phi_{a,x}\rangle
 =\frac{|a;R\rangle-|a;L\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\end{equation}
Direct differentiation of \eqref{eq:bond-peierls} gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_A(\sqrt{j_a}\Pi_{a,x}(A))|_0|a\rangle
 =\I\sqrt{\frac{2j_a}{V}}\,|\phi_{a,x}\rangle,
 \label{eq:swap-source}
\end{equation}
and it annihilates the one-pair state in the other block.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_x\Pi_{a,x}|\phi_{a,x}\rangle
 \propto\sum_x(|a;x+1\rangle-|a;x\rangle)=0.
\end{equation}
Thus the stacked swap source lies in $\ker D(0)^\dagger$ and requires no
least-squares subtraction.  Its curvature contribution is
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm swap}^{(1)}
 =2\begin{pmatrix}j_1&0\\0&j_2\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:Q-swap}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cross-row source}

Choose normalized split-singlet states $|\chi_x\rangle$ on each bond so that,
after a harmless phase convention,
\begin{equation}
 \partial_A(gK_x(A))|_0|1\rangle
 =\frac{2\I g}{\sqrt V}|\chi_x\rangle,
 \qquad
 \partial_A(gK_x(A))|_0|2\rangle
 =-\frac{2\I g}{\sqrt V}|\chi_x\rangle.
 \label{eq:cross-source}
\end{equation}
One explicit choice is
\begin{align}
 |\chi_x\rangle=\frac12\bigl(&
 c_{1,R,\uparrow}^\dagger c_{2,L,\downarrow}^\dagger
 -c_{1,R,\downarrow}^\dagger c_{2,L,\uparrow}^\dagger\nonumber\\
 &-c_{1,L,\uparrow}^\dagger c_{2,R,\downarrow}^\dagger
 +c_{1,L,\downarrow}^\dagger c_{2,R,\uparrow}^\dagger
 \bigr)|0\rangle.
 \label{eq:chi-state}
\end{align}
The local action of $K_x$ on this state is proportional to
\begin{equation}
 (|1;R\rangle-|1;L\rangle)
 -( |2;R\rangle-|2;L\rangle).
 \label{eq:K-on-chi}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:K-on-chi} over the ring telescopes to zero.  Hence the
stacked cross source also lies in $\ker D(0)^\dagger$.  Since the factor-row
labels are orthogonal in the target, \eqref{eq:cross-source} gives
\begin{equation}
 Q_K^{(1)}
 =4g^2
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&-1\\
 -1&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:Q-cross}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact one-pair composition operator]
\label{thm:escape-Q}
The exact second-order operator is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 Q^{(1)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2j_1+4g^2&-4g^2\\
 -4g^2&2j_2+4g^2
 \end{pmatrix}.}
 \label{eq:exact-Q}
\end{equation}
The sign of the off-diagonal entry changes under a phase reversal of one
composition basis vector, but its magnitude does not.  Its eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \lambda_\pm
 =j_1+j_2+4g^2
 \pm\sqrt{(j_1-j_2)^2+16g^4}.}
 \label{eq:Q-eigs}
\end{equation}
For $j_1,j_2>0$ and $g\ne0$, $\lambda_->0$ and
$(Q^{(1)})_{12}\ne0$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 E_\pm(A)=\frac{A^2}{2}\lambda_\pm+o(A^2),
\end{equation}
and the analytic curvature eigenbranches are coherent superpositions of the
two block compositions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The swap and cross factor labels occupy orthogonal summands of the target
space, so their projected Gram matrices add.  Equations
\eqref{eq:Q-swap} and \eqref{eq:Q-cross} give \eqref{eq:exact-Q}.
Diagonalization gives \eqref{eq:Q-eigs}.  Positivity of the lower eigenvalue
follows from
\begin{equation}
 (j_1+j_2+4g^2)^2-\bigl[(j_1-j_2)^2+16g^4\bigr]
 =4j_1j_2+8g^2(j_1+j_2)>0.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Extensive-filling scaling]
The one-pair hopping $(Q^{(1)})_{12}=-4g^2$ is independent of $V$, and the
finite verifier also finds nonzero off-diagonal response in the $V=3$, $n=2$
composition sector.  We make no thermodynamic claim here at fixed nonzero
pair density $n/V$.  Determining whether an off-diagonal curvature density
survives, vanishes, or requires a coupling rescaling in extensive sectors is a
separate open problem.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharpness and interpretation}

The theorem and escape model meet at an exact algebraic boundary:

The many-body row $K_x=\Pi_{12,x}B_x$ is the minimal structural escape because

realize that pattern.

\section{Conclusion}

The physical two-block program has a sharp answer.  Exact local one-body
projected-Hubbard or QGN parents with isolated interaction subbundles cannot
acquire composition hopping from a complete electromagnetic Peierls twist.
Their apparent off-block motion is common subbundle transport and is
eliminated by the frustration-free least-squares projection.

A local many-body multiplicative null row is the minimal exact escape in
hypothesis space.  It can hide interblock transfer at zero twist, retain a
completely auditable product-AGP zero manifold, and produce a fully covariant
non-diagonal composition-space response.  The failed search for a final
one-body example is therefore replaced by a structural theorem and by the
first exact model beyond its boundary.
